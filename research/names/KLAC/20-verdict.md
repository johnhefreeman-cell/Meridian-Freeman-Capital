## The one-line finding

**KLA is a genuinely excellent business priced as something it is not.** 61.3%
gross margin, 77% pre-tax ROIC, 0.16x net leverage, twelve straight quarters at
or above the guided midpoint, and a PwC signature unbroken since 1977 — and it
returns 88.9% of free cash flow to shareholders, which under §3.1 makes it a
**capital-return story to be underwritten at a FCF yield**. That yield is
**1.55%**. And the bear's *base* case — the guide holds, nothing impairs, the
multiple merely reverts to KLA's own four-year median — still loses **33.6%**.

## Gate table — §3.1, Type B column

| # | Gate | Type B bar | Score | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Revenue durability | ≥50% repeat, or aftermarket ≥30% of gross profit | **FAIL** | Service + refurb 24% of revenue; service gross profit ≤23% of total even imputed at corporate margin. Service GM is not disclosed. |
| 2 | Unit economics | GM ≥30% **and** ROIC ≥15% | **PASS** | GM 61.3%; EBIT $5,660.8M on invested capital $7,334.8M = 77% pre-tax |
| 3 | Earnings quality | FCF/NI ≥0.8, 3-yr avg | **PASS (0.93)** | FY26 standalone **0.78 fails**; 0.72 adjusted for the factoring step-change. The trend is the finding, not the average. |
| 4 | Balance sheet | Net debt/EBITDA ≤3.0x, no 24-mo wall | **PASS** | 0.16x; first maturity FY2029; covenant 0.53x against a 3.25x max |
| 5 | Incentives | Insider ≥3% or named operator; comp tied to per-share value | **FAIL** | Insiders **0.0915%**. Bonus on "Operating Margin Dollar" — adjusted operating income in absolute dollars, no per-share link. Zero externally verifiable metrics; no relative TSR. |
| 6 | Reinvestment runway | ≥50% of FCF redeployable at ≥ current ROIC | **FAIL** | $3,347.6M of $3,767.1M FCF (88.9%) to dividends and buybacks; capex $375.9M (10.0%); acquisitions $0 |

**Three of six fail.** §3.1 requires all six for a full workup.

Gate 6 is the one that decides the name. A business returning 89% of FCF is not
a compounder, and §3.1 is explicit about what follows: *"It may still be a
capital-return story — but then it is underwritten as one, at a FCF yield, and
it does not get compounder multiples."* KLAC trades at 50.7x.

## §5 — the asymmetry test, and why it is decisive

§5 requires a base-case 3-year IRR ≥15% **and** a bear case losing **<25%**.

Both limbs fail, and the two agents built their scenarios independently.

| Whose case | Assumption | Exit | 3-yr outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bull, base | FY30E EPS $7.94, exit at the 5-yr median +35% | 28.0x | **+6.7% IRR** |
| Bull, at the 5-yr median itself | §5's default terminal cap | 20.7x | **−3.4% IRR** |
| **Bear, base — the guide holds, nothing impairs** | FY27 +25%, FY28 +8%, one modest digestion year; multiple reverts to KLA's own 4-yr median | 24.5x | **−33.6%** total, −12.7% p.a. |
| Bear, bear | AI capex digests one year: FY28 revenue −25%, GM to 57.5%, multiple below the FY24–26 band | 22.0x | **−64.1%** total, −28.8% p.a. |
| Bear, bull | Process-control intensity structurally steps up, China eases, market pays the board's best-ever multiple | 34.5x | +33.8% total, **+10.2% p.a.** |

- **Base-case IRR ≥15%: fails on both agents' arithmetic.** The bull's own
  defensible base is +6.7%; even its optimistic 34.5x exit is +10.2% p.a.
- **Bear loses <25%: fails at −64.1%**, and the limit is breached by more than
  two and a half times.

**The finding is the row in the middle.** The bear's *base* case assumes no
missed quarter, no impairment, no lost customer — the guide holds — and the
multiple simply reverts to KLA's own four-year median. It still loses **33.6%**.
§5's downside limit is breached with nothing going wrong in the business.

Break-even over three years requires a **37.9x** exit; a 15% IRR requires
**56.9x** — above every observation in either primary-source series, and above
today's 50.7x trailing, which is itself the highest in the record. (Two multiple
bases appear across the files and both are stated: **50.7x on trailing FY26 EPS
of $3.66**, and **38.5x on the bull's FY27E**. Neither is a vendor figure.)

**The guidance dissent, on the record.** The criterion is *"missed its own
guidance 3+ times in 8 quarters and has not changed the guidance framework."*
The trailing eight quarters contain **1** GAAP EPS miss. The historical window
1Q24–4Q25 contains **3**, and KLA has never changed its framework — so on an
"any 8-quarter window" reading the criterion trips. All three misses are
non-cash PCB/Display impairments ($219.0M, $70.5M, $239.1M) in a segment that
was 5.6% of FY24 revenue and has since been exited; revenue and non-GAAP EPS
beat the midpoint in each of those quarters. **This is the same window-definition
ambiguity raised on AMAT and it is still unruled.** Recorded, not killed on.

## The disconfirming evidence, recorded as prominently as the case

This is the strongest counter-evidence found on any of the three semis names,
and §7 requires it be shown rather than netted away:

- **There is no guide-down behind this drawdown.** The 2026-07-28 8-K guides
  September-quarter revenue **up 24.6% YoY**, with the CEO calling momentum
  "accelerating."
- KLA beat the revenue and GAAP EPS midpoints in each of the last three quarters,
  and 47 of 48 metric-quarter pairs over three years landed at or above the guide.
- Growth is entirely organic — acquisitions $0.
- The GAAP-to-non-GAAP EPS gap is **2.7%**. Add-backs are 3.3–7.3% of opex.
- Auditor unbroken since 1977; ICFR effective.
- The June 2022 Investor Day EPS promise was **delivered**.

**The business is accelerating while the stock has fallen 40%.** The bear case
here is a price case, not a business case, and the file says so.

  our backlog, requiring us to return substantial deposits."
- FY26 FCF/NI of **0.78** fails gate 3 standalone; the 3-year average passes.

## The comp calibration — worth the PM's attention independent of the verdict

KLA delivered its four-year public EPS promise to within **0.3%** and will pay
**~149% of target** for it, because (a) every FY23 EPS Award goal was set
**6.5%–9.4% below the low end of the path KLA publicly promised** at Investor
Day; (b) in **August 2024** the committee certified FY22 PRSUs at the **82nd
percentile** and in the *same month* set the new maximum-payout gate at the
**80th** while raising max payout from 150% to 200%; (c) the CY2023 bonus target
was cut **30% below the prior year's actual** and paid **118% for a 21.8% YoY
decline**; (d) the largest single scorecard weight, **40%**, sits on
unverifiable self-assessed objectives.

The method behind (a) validates: it reproduces the company's own certified 131%
and 147% payouts.

That is a structural transfer of roughly a third to a half of the long-term
equity award. **Size it in the dilution assumption; it is not a governance
footnote.**

## Limitations, stated

- **Price and the 52-week high come from the holder's brokerage workbook, not a
  filing.** Share count, EPS, buyback prices and cover-page market values are all
  from filings; the current price is not.
- **Average daily dollar volume is unverified** — the `market` MCP server is
  down. The §2.1 liquidity ceiling is therefore *asserted* non-binding at
  KLA's size rather than computed. On a $242.5bn name this is a formality, but
  it is an uncomputed one and is labelled as such.
- The 10-for-1 split of 2026-06-11 is handled throughout: guidance is scored on
  a pre-split basis, and the workbook price series was independently confirmed
  split-adjusted (largest weekly move in five years is −16.2%; an unadjusted
  split would print −90%).
