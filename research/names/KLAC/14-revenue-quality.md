# KLAC — Revenue Quality

**Business type: B · Asset-heavy operating** (assigned in `00-base-facts.md`; not re-derived).
Fiscal year ends 30 June. FY26 = 2025-07-01 → 2026-06-30. USD millions, one decimal.

---

## Conclusion first

KLA's revenue is **real and organic**, and the customer-concentration kill criterion is
**not tripped**. But **gate 1 does not pass**, and **gate 3 passes only on the three-year
average while the FY26 print is below the line and falling**.

Three findings drive that:

1. **TSMC is 19% of revenue in each of FY26 and FY25, and the customer is named in the
   filing.** Below the 25% §4 threshold. But the contract limb is empty: KLA's orders are
   "generally subject to cancellation or delay by the customer with limited or no penalty"
   `[10-K FY26, acc 0000319201-26-000027, Item 1A]`. Same structural fact as AMAT — the
   percentage clears, the contract does not exist. KLA differs from AMAT in that it **did
   not delete the customer names**.
2. **Service gross profit is not disclosed anywhere in KLA's filings**, and an upper bound
   proves the aftermarket limb of gate 1 fails. Service is 23.0% of revenue. For service to
   be 30% of gross profit, service gross margin would have to be **79.9%** — above the
   corporate 61.3%. It cannot be. Gate 1 aftermarket limb: **FAIL**.
3. **Cash conversion is deteriorating on a clean trend.** FCF/NI 1.10 → 0.92 → **0.78**.
   4Q26 DSO 71.9 days, 76.4 including a brand-new $180.7M long-term receivable, **82.1
   including receivables factored in the quarter [EST]** — versus 67.4 a year earlier. And
   the receivables-concentration-by-customer table was **deleted** from the FY25 and FY26
   10-Ks after appearing in FY22–FY24.

**Read on the AMAT comparison.** On AMAT the customer names had been deleted from the FY25
10-K. **KLA has not deleted them.** "Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited"
appears in the FY26 10-K in both Item 1 and MD&A. Samsung fell off the list after FY23 —
that is a genuine decline below 10%, not a redaction, because the count of names and the
count of percentages move together (two names / two percentages FY20–FY23; one name / one
percentage FY24–FY26).

**Peak two-customer concentration was FY23 at ~33% combined (18% + 15%).** That is the
number to carry into any downside case, not the current 19%.

### 1.2 The contract limb — empty

§4 kills at >25% from one customer **with no multi-year contract**. At 19% the percentage
limb is not met, so the criterion is not tripped. The contract limb is nonetheless worth
establishing, because it is what would make a move from 19% to 26% fatal rather than merely
uncomfortable.

There is **no multi-year supply contract with TSMC or any customer** disclosed in the FY26
10-K. What exists instead:

- **Cancellable purchase orders.** *"...a decrease in, or cancellation or delay of, orders
  (which are generally subject to cancellation or delay by the customer with limited or no
  penalty)"* `[10-K FY26, Item 1A, Risk Factors]`.
- **Volume purchase agreements** — these are price/credit mechanisms, not volume
  commitments: *"we occasionally enter into volume purchase agreements with our larger
  customers, and these agreements may provide for certain volume purchase incentives, such
  as credits toward future purchases"* `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]`. They oblige KLA, not the
  customer. They also reduce recognised revenue via variable-consideration accruals (§2).
- **Backlog is not a contract.** $12.57B at 2026-06-30 vs $7.86B at 2025-06-30
  `[10-K FY26, Item 1 "Backlog"]`, but KLA states *"there is increased risk of order
  modifications, pushouts or cancellations"* and *"Our backlog on any particular date does
  not provide meaningful information about the timing of future revenue recognition."*
- KLA **did** return customer deposits on cancelled China purchase orders — proof the
  cancellation right is exercised in practice, not theoretical `[10-K FY26, Item 1A,
  tariffs risk factor: "we have had to return deposits given to us by our customers upon
  cancellation of their purchase orders"]`. Customer deposits fell from $636.4M to $430.1M
  over FY26 `[10-K FY26, Note 4]`.

**Verdict: percentage 19% < 25% → §4 A/B criterion NOT tripped. Contract term: none exists.
Margin of safety is 6 percentage points of revenue and nothing else.**

### 1.3 Receivables concentration — the disclosure that was deleted

KLA disclosed >10%-of-net-accounts-receivable customers **by name** in the FY22, FY23 and
FY24 10-Ks. Example, FY24: TSMC and Samsung both >10% of net AR `[10-K FY24, acc
0000319201-24-000021, Note 1]`.

**That table does not appear in the FY25 or FY26 10-K.** Verified by full-text search of
both documents for "10% of net accounts receivable": present in FY22/FY23/FY24, absent in
FY25/FY26.

This matters because it was removed in the same window in which accounts receivable grew
28% on 12% revenue growth, a $180.7M long-term receivable appeared from nil, and factored
receivables more than doubled. We no longer know which customers owe KLA the money, at the
moment the receivable balance became the most interesting item on the balance sheet. This
is the KLA analogue of the AMAT name deletion — different line item, same direction.

## 2. Recognition policy and judgments

Policy note: `[10-K FY26, acc 0000319201-26-000027, Note 1 "Revenue Recognition", p.58–59]`.

**Revenue recognition is the sole Critical Audit Matter** in PwC's FY26 report `[10-K FY26,
Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm, p.93]`. PwC's stated reason is
*"a high degree of auditor effort"* — effort, not estimation uncertainty. That is the
milder of the two CAM formulations. PwC has audited KLA since 1977; PCAOB ID 238; opinion
unqualified on both statements and ICFR.

Judgments that matter, ranked:

1. **Point-in-time product revenue on a control-transfer judgment, with acceptance
   sometimes treated as a formality.** Product is 77.0% of revenue and is recognised at a
   point in time. KLA lists five control indicators and states *"Not all of the indicators
   need to be met for us to conclude that control has transferred."* Acceptance can be
   deemed perfunctory *"when the customer has previously accepted the same tool, with the
   same specifications or technology."* Where revenue is booked before acceptance, only the
   fair value of the **installation** obligation is deferred. **This is the largest single
   judgment in the P&L and it is the reason $932.9M of deferred system revenue exists.**
2. **Variable consideration from volume purchase agreements.** *"We adjust the transaction
   consideration for estimated credits and incentives earned by our customers... estimated
   based upon the forecasted and actual product sales."* KLA warns this *"could materially
   and adversely affect our results of operations in near-term periods, including the
   revenue we can recognize on product sales"* `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]`. The accrual balance
   is **not disclosed**. This is an undisclosed contra-revenue estimate on the largest
   customers.
3. **Standalone selling price allocation** across bundled product / warranty / extended
   warranty / PCS performance obligations, using *"historical stand-alone sales,
   discounting strategies and other observable data."*
4. **Warranty as a separate performance obligation.** The standard 12-month warranty is
   unbundled from the tool and its estimated fair value deferred and recognised ratably.
   This shifts revenue from product to service over time and is a judgment about fair
   value, not an observable price.

**No percentage-of-completion.** No principal-vs-agent / gross-vs-net judgment disclosed.
**Significant financing component: explicitly not adjusted for** — practical expedient
elected on the basis that the pay period is *"generally one year or less."* That expedient
is now in tension with the new $180.7M **long-term** accounts receivable (§4), which by
definition is not collectible within a year. The 10-K does not reconcile the two.

**Change in policy or estimate vs prior year: none disclosed.** Policy language is
substantively identical to FY25. No restatement. No material weakness. Item 9A clean.

## 3. Durability evidence — gate 1, Type B column

Gate 1 for Type B: **≥50% repeat, OR aftermarket attach ≥30% of gross profit.**

### 3.1 The aftermarket limb — FAIL, on an upper bound

**KLA does not disclose service gross profit or service gross margin.** Verified three ways:

- The income statement carries **one** `Costs of revenues` line — $5,255.1M FY26 — with no
  product/service split, despite revenue being split into Product and Service one line
  above `[10-K FY26, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p.50]`.
- The segment note splits costs of revenues by **reportable segment** (Semiconductor
  Process Control / Specialty Semiconductor Process / PCB and Component Inspection), not by
  product vs service; and KLA states *"Services are offered in multiple segments"*
  `[10-K FY26, Note 17, p.89–91]`.
- XBRL confirms it: the only cost-of-sales tag KLA reports is `us-gaap:CostOfRevenue`
  (single aggregate). There is no `CostOfServices` / `CostOfGoodsSold` pair. Full-text
  search of EDGAR 2023-present for "service gross margin" returns **zero KLA filings**.

So the limb cannot be scored from disclosure. It **can** be bounded, and the bound is
decisive.

FY26 gross profit = 61.3% × $13,579.5M = **$8,324.2M** `[10-K FY26, MD&A p.39: gross margin
61.3%, 60.9%, 60.0%]`. Service revenue = **$3,125.9M = 23.02%** of total.

| Assumed service gross margin | Implied service share of gross profit |
| --- | --- |
| 35% | 13.1% |
| 40% | 15.0% |
| 45% | 16.9% |
| 50% | 18.8% |
| 61.3% (= corporate, i.e. service exactly as profitable as tools) | **23.0%** |
| **79.9% — required to hit the 30% gate** | **30.0%** |

Source: computed from `[10-K FY26, Consolidated Statements of Operations p.50; MD&A p.39]`.
Method: service GP share = (service revenue × assumed service GM) ÷ (total revenue ×
61.3%). Labelled `[EST]` — the assumed service GM is an assumption, the revenue split and
the corporate gross margin are reported.

**Even the absurd ceiling case — service exactly as profitable as a $6.6bn wafer-inspection
tool franchise — yields 23.0%, seven points short of the 30% gate.** Clearing the gate
requires a 79.9% service gross margin on a business that carries $622.7M of customer
service parts inventory `[10-K FY26, Note 4]` and runs a global field engineering
organisation KLA describes as a competitive advantage in headcount terms `[Item 1, "Sales,
Service and Marketing"]`. It is not credible.

**Aftermarket limb: FAIL. Not UNKNOWN — the upper bound alone fails it.**

### 3.2 The repeat limb — UNKNOWN

KLA discloses no repeat-revenue percentage, no installed-base unit count, no service
contract attach rate, and no service contract renewal rate. What exists is qualitative:

- *"once a semiconductor manufacturer selects a particular supplier's process control and
  yield management equipment, the manufacturer generally relies upon that equipment for
  that specific production line application for an extended period of time"* `[10-K FY26,
  Item 1A]`.
- *"The amount of our service revenues is typically a function of the number of systems
  installed at our customers' sites and the utilization of those systems, but it is also
  impacted by... **our rate of service contract renewals**"* `[10-K FY26, MD&A p.40]` —
  KLA names the renewal rate as a driver **and never states it.**

Quantifiable repeat/installed-base revenue = Services 23.0% + Other 1.1% (refurbished
systems, remanufactured legacy systems, upgrades to previous-generation products, per Note
17) = **24.1%**. Product revenue to returning customers is not disclosed.

**Repeat limb: UNKNOWN. 24.1% is the floor we can tie to a filing; 50% cannot be
established.**

### 3.3 The company's own durability language — quoted, not adopted

*"our services business... accounted for approximately 23% of our total revenues in fiscal
2026. Our services revenue, which is generated largely from recurring 'subscription-like'
contracts..."* `[10-K FY26, MD&A Executive Summary, p.36]`.

**"Subscription-like" is a label with no supporting disclosure.** There is no renewal rate,
no contract duration, no attach rate, no net retention figure anywhere in the filing.
Per §7 the label is quoted and not adopted. Note also that a material portion of "service"
revenue is not subscription at all — it is *"billable time and material service calls"* and
spare-parts sales recognised on transfer of control `[Note 1; MD&A p.40]`.

| 2023-06-30 | 11,400 |

Backlog is 92.6% of FY26 revenue and grew 60% in FY26 on AI infrastructure demand. It is a

### 3.5 Gate 1 verdict

> **GATE 1 (Type B): FAIL.**
> Aftermarket limb FAIL — service is 23.0% of revenue and, at any credible service gross
> margin, 13–19% of gross profit; the 30% threshold requires a 79.9% service gross margin
> `[10-K FY26, acc 0000319201-26-000027, p.39, p.50, Note 17]`.
> Repeat limb UNKNOWN — 24.1% is the disclosed floor; ≥50% cannot be established from any
> filing. Per CLAUDE.md §3.1, UNKNOWN is not a pass.

### 3.6 Read across to AMAT — the taxonomy question

| | AMAT (per prior workup) | KLA (this workup) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service revenue share | 22.5% | **23.0%** |
| Service gross profit share | 13–17% (disclosed) | **13–19% [EST]** (not disclosed) |
| Customer names in latest 10-K | **deleted** | **retained** |
| Largest customer | 19–20% | **19%** |
| Multi-year customer contract | none | **none** |
| Gate 1 Type B | FAIL | **FAIL** |

The hypothesis that KLA's service business is structurally larger than AMAT's **is false**.
23.0% vs 22.5% of revenue is a rounding difference, and the gross-profit share lands in the
same 13–19% band. KLA is in fact the **worse** disclosure of the two: AMAT at least
publishes the split.

**This is now two of two large-cap semicap names failing Type B gate 1 on the same
mechanism** — service revenue in the low 20s, service gross profit in the low-to-mid teens,
because the tool carries the margin and the service does not. The failure is not
company-specific; it is what a process-control business *is*. A capital-equipment franchise
with a ~90% backlog-to-revenue ratio, a 61% gross margin, a 35% net margin and no
contracted revenue does not fit column B ("repeat/aftermarket revenue, capital in the
ground") any better than it fits column A or D. **Recommend the taxonomy question be
escalated: semicap looks like a fifth column, not a Type B.** The distinguishing economics
are cycle-amplitude and installed-base lock-in without contracted revenue — neither of
which any existing column tests.

### What broke in FY26

FY26 OCF grew **1.5%** while net income grew **18.9%**. The working-capital bridge
`[10-K FY26, p.52]`:

| Working capital item | FY26 | FY25 | FY24 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accounts receivable | (641.8) | (367.9) | (80.9) |
| Inventories | (466.0) | (155.2) | (164.1) |
| Other assets | **(501.4)** | (10.5) | (289.5) |
| Accounts payable | 172.4 | 33.8 | 25.0 |
| Deferred system revenue | 116.1 | (169.0) | 334.1 |
| Deferred service revenue | (54.6) | 100.5 | 203.1 |

Four tells, in order of severity:

1. **AR grew 27.6% on 11.7% revenue growth** — $2,263.9M → $2,889.2M `[Note 4]`. Gross AR
   $2,920.2M, allowance $31.0M (allowance actually *fell* from $34.0M).
2. **A $180.7M long-term receivable appeared from nil.** `[10-K FY26, Note 4 "Other
   non-current assets"]`. KLA gives no explanation, no counterparty, and no payment terms.
   It sits in a company that elected the ASC 606 practical expedient on the grounds that
   payment periods are *"generally one year or less."* Including it, 4Q26 DSO is 76.4 days.
3. **Receivables factored more than doubled: $515.5M FY26 vs $230.6M FY25 vs $254.9M FY24**
   `[10-K FY26, MD&A "Factoring Arrangements" p.44 and Note 15]`. 4Q26 alone was **$229.3M**
   — more than the entire prior fiscal year — against $87.1M in 4Q25. Factoring is
   without recourse, so the proceeds land in operating cash flow and the receivable leaves
   the balance sheet. **The reported 4Q26 DSO of 71.9 days is already after $229.3M of
   receivables were removed by sale.** Adding them back: 82.1 days `[EST]`, vs 67.4 a year
   earlier — **+14.7 days**, roughly $590M of revenue that did not convert to cash.
4. **Deferred revenue is flat while revenue compounds.** Contract liabilities $1,775.1M vs
   $1,713.7M vs $1,782.2M across FY26/25/24 — +3.6% over two years on +38.4% revenue. The
   deferred balance is *not* funding growth; growth is being financed out of receivables.

Deferred revenue runoff: $1,537.0M of the $1,775.1M is expected in revenue within 12
months, $163.4M in year two, $74.8M thereafter `[10-K FY26, Note 2]`. $1.29B of the FY25
opening balance was recognised in FY26.

**Net read: the top line is real — it is invoiced, and the revenue-recognition CAM is a
low-severity one — but KLA is increasingly funding its customers to book it.** Rising DSO,
a new long-term receivable, and a doubling of factoring are three independent ways of
saying the same thing. This is the single most important item for the next two quarters.

## 6. China and export controls

- **China = 29.8% of FY26 revenue** (33.3% FY25, 42.8% FY24). KLA restates it in Item 1A as
  *"30%, 33% and 43%"* `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]`.
- Applicable regimes disclosed: **2022 BIS Rules** (Oct 2022), **2024 and 2025 BIS Rules**
  (Dec 2024 / Jan 2025, expanding the advanced-DRAM definition), and the **"Affiliates
  Rule"** interim final rule of **September 2025**, extending controls to foreign entities
  ≥50% owned by Entity List / MEU / SDN parties `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]`.
- Operational impact is already realised, not prospective: *"some of our products destined
  for China have been held up by U.S. Customs and Border Protection"*; *"Failure to obtain
  export licenses has harmed and could continue to harm our backlog, requiring us to return
  substantial deposits received from customers in China for purchase orders"* `[Item 1A]`.
  Customer deposits fell $206.2M over FY26.
- **Section 232 semiconductor investigation** initiated April 2025, scope explicitly
  includes semiconductor manufacturing equipment `[Item 1A]`.
- Reverse direction: Chinese rare-earth export controls (April 2025, expanded October and
  November 2025) hit KLA's own supply chain `[Item 1A]`.
- KLA states plainly: *"future revenue from China as a percentage of our overall revenue
  may decline as a result of the current and future Commerce rules and regulations."*

The China share has fallen 13 points in two years while total revenue grew 38%. The
absolute China dollar figure is flat ($4,196.7 → $4,042.6 → $4,048.4). **The China risk is
already partially realised and has been absorbed by Taiwan/Korea/North America growth. The
un-modelled risk is the *service* annuity on the China installed base**, which is exactly
what the Affiliates Rule reaches and which KLA does not size.
