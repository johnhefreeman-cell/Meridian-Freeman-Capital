# KLAC — Bull Case

**Author:** bull agent · **Date:** 2026-09-05 · **Price used:** $185.60 (base-facts file)
**Business type: B · Asset-heavy operating** (Semiconductor Process Control = 90.2% of FY26
revenue; the other two segments are each <6%, so no segment crosses the §3.0 25%-of-earnings

## Conclusion first

**The business clears five of six gates and is one of the highest-quality industrials in the
book: 61.3% gross margin, 41.7% operating margin, 66.5% ROIC, 0.17x net leverage, and a
guided +25% YoY quarter in front of it. The price does not clear. At $185.60 KLAC trades at
38.5x the fiscal year in progress against a five-year median of 20.7x on the same measure,
computed from the company's own cover-page disclosures. Even on a bull revenue path — 20%
revenue CAGR for three years, 38% net margin, 1.2%/yr share shrink, EPS compounding 21.4% —
the 3-year IRR is +6.7% at a 28x exit and −3.4% at the five-year median exit. The bull case
clears the fund's 15% hurdle only if the market pays ~38x forward in September 2029, which is
1.9x the five-year median and which I cannot justify from any disclosure. I am reporting that
rather than reverse-engineering an exit multiple to make the memo work.**

The buy price at which this bull path clears 15% is **$148 (28x exit)** or **$110 (20.7x
median exit)**. That is 20–41% below today.

## 1. Mechanism — one sentence

**KLA converts each new leading-edge node into a permanent step-up in process-control
intensity: because inspection and metrology insertions scale with the number of critical
layers rather than with wafer starts, KLA's revenue grows faster than the fabs it sells into,
and it does so at a 64.6% incremental gross margin because the tool is an optics/algorithm
franchise with no second source at the leading edge.**

That is the *only* mechanism I will defend. Two candidate mechanisms I tested and **reject**
as load-bearing are in §3 (service annuity; net-margin inflection). Naming three would mean
having none.

### The §5 rules, applied honestly

- **Multiple expansion ≤40% of return.** The only case that clears 15% (38.5x flat) contains
  **0% multiple expansion** — it passes the letter of the rule. But it passes only because
  the entry multiple is already extreme. Passing a no-expansion test from a 38.5x base is not
  the same thing as the rule being satisfied in spirit, and I am flagging that rather than
  hiding behind it.
- **Terminal multiple at or below the trailing 5-year median.** 20.7x. My bull case at 20.7x
  returns **−3.4%/yr**. To justify anything above it I must name a structural change. The
  best candidate I can evidence is the China de-risking in §3 assumption 6. It is real but it
  is worth a few turns, not eighteen.
- **Base IRR ≥15% and bear loses <25%.** Not met on any exit multiple I can defend.

### The reverse question: what does $185.60 already require?

At a 28x exit and a 15% hurdle, FY30 EPS must be **$9.97** → net income $12.5B → revenue
**$33.0B** at a 38% net margin → a **24.8% revenue CAGR for four straight years** from FY26.
KLA has posted +24.0% (FY25) and +11.7% (FY26). Four consecutive years at 25% would be
unprecedented in the disclosed history and is not supported by anything in the filings. It is
the market's implied forecast, not mine.

## 3. Evidence, assumption by assumption

**Assumption 1 — FY27 revenue $17.0B (+25.2%). SUPPORTED for one quarter, `[EST]` beyond.**
Q1 FY27 guidance is $4.00B ±$0.20B, versus Q1 FY26 actual $3,209.7M — **+24.6% YoY at the
midpoint** [8-K 2026-07-28, accn `0000319201-26-000024`, Ex-99.1]. Management: "we see
momentum across our business accelerating in the second half of calendar 2026 and continuing
through 2027" (Wallace, same release), and "Looking ahead to fiscal year 2027, we expect
continued revenue growth as customer engagement and demand signals continue to strengthen"
[10-K FY26, Executive Summary]. My $17.0B holds the guided Q1 and steps quarters up ~3.5%
sequentially. **KLA does not disclose backlog or remaining performance obligations.** The only
forward contractual figure is contract liabilities of $1,775.1M, of which $1,537.0M is
expected to convert within 12 months [10-K FY26, revenue note] — 11.3% of FY26 revenue. There
is no disclosure that supports a multi-year revenue forecast. FY28–FY30 are `[EST]` decay from
the guided rate and are **unsupported by any filing**.

**Assumption 2 — 38% net margin. SUPPORTED as a near-term level; UNSUPPORTED as expansion.**
Q1 FY27 guidance implies a 37.3% net margin (GAAP EPS $1.14 × ~1,310M shares / $4.00B), above
the FY26 actual 35.6%. Gross margin has risen three straight years: 60.0% → 60.9% → 61.3%
[10-K FY26 MD&A], with Q1 FY27 guided at 61.6% ±1.0%. Tax rate is 13.8% (775.2/5,605.9).
**But the disclosed gross-margin bridge attributes the FY25→FY26 gain to volume (+0.5pt) and
manufacturing efficiency (+0.2pt), with mix a −0.2pt drag** [10-K FY26 MD&A, gross margin
bridge]. Operating leverage is thinner than the headline: R&D grew 12.6% and SG&A 9.9% against
revenue +11.7%, so incremental operating margin ex-impairment was 45.4% versus a 41.7% average.
And ex-goodwill-impairment the margin path is far flatter than it looks: FY24 ~31.0% `[EST]`
(adding back the $289.5M impairment at the 13.8% marginal rate), FY25 35.4%, FY26 35.6%. **The
margin inflection has already happened.** I hold 38% rather than model further expansion.

**Assumption 3 — Service is NOT the mechanism. This is a negative finding and it matters.**
The task asked me to test KLA against the AMAT standard honestly. It fails the same test, and
in one respect fails it harder:

| Test | KLA FY26 | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service revenue | $3,125.9M, 23.0% of total, +16.5% YoY | Disclosed [10-K FY26 income stmt] |
| Service **gross margin** | **Not disclosed.** KLA reports one "Costs of revenues" line; there is no product/service cost split anywhere in the 10-K. | **Cannot be computed at all** — worse than AMAT, where the split exists |
| Service mix trend | 23.7% (FY24) → 22.1% (FY25) → 23.0% (FY26) | **Flat.** Service is not gaining mix, so it cannot be driving margin |
| Contractual proportion | "generated largely from recurring 'subscription-like' contracts" [10-K FY26, Executive Summary] — but also "billable time and material service calls" [MD&A] with no split given | Management assertion, unquantified |
| Renewal rate | Referenced ("our rate of service contract renewals") but **never quantified** | Not disclosed |
| Deferred service revenue | $604.1M current + $238.1M non-current = $842.2M = 26.9% of one year of service revenue | Weak contractual anchor |

**Conclusion: the "installed-base service annuity" story is not supportable from KLA's
filings.** Service grows with the installed base — that is disclosed and true — but it is not
gaining mix, its margin is undisclosed, and its contractual fraction is undisclosed. A bull
case built on it would be built on a management adjective. Gate 1 for Type B (≥50% repeat, or
aftermarket attach ≥30% of gross profit) is therefore scored **UNKNOWN, not PASS**: service is
23.0% of revenue and its gross-profit contribution cannot be computed.

**Assumption 4 — FCF conversion 0.85. SUPPORTED on a 3-year average, deteriorating on trend.**
FY24 1.10, FY25 0.92, **FY26 0.78** [OCF and capex per 10-K FY26 cash flow statement: OCF
$4,143.1M, capex $375.9M, FCF $3,767.1M, matching the 8-K's "$3.77 billion"]. Three-year
average 0.93 → Gate 3 PASS. The FY26 fall to 0.78 is working capital: receivables +$586.5M and
inventories +$212.3M in Q4 alone [8-K Ex-99.1 cash flow statement]. In a 20%-growth path
working capital consumes cash, so 0.85 is the right number to use, not 0.93.

**Assumption 5 — 1.2%/yr share shrink. SUPPORTED.** Diluted shares 1,361.9M (FY24) → 1,337.5M
(FY25) → 1,319.6M (FY26), −1.3% in FY26. FY26 buyback $2,289.8M and dividends $1,057.8M

at 2026-06-30, after the Board **added $7.00B on 2026-03-11** — when the stock was
approximately $170 (April repurchases averaged $170.47). [10-K FY26 Item 5 and cash flow
statement.] Share count is falling, not rising: the §4 kill criterion on dilution is clear.

**Assumption 6 — the exit multiple. THIS IS THE LOAD-BEARING ASSUMPTION AND IT IS THE WEAKEST.**
The convention throughout is price ÷ diluted EPS of the fiscal year in progress, applied
identically at every historical date and at exit, so entry and exit are on the same footing.
The only structural change I can evidence that argues for a terminal multiple above the 20.7x
median is **the China de-risking**: revenue from China fell from 42.8% of total (FY24) to
33.3% (FY25) to **29.8% (FY26)** [10-K FY26, revenues by region; restated in the risk factor
as "30%, 33% and 43%"]. During FY23–FY25 the group carried an export-control discount; that
exposure has shrunk by 13 points of revenue while total revenue grew 38%. This is real,
sourced, and worth some multiple. It is not worth the 18 turns between 20.7x and 38.5x. **I
cannot evidence the exit multiple that the bull case needs. That is the honest state of this
thesis.**

## 5. Steelmanned bear points, and my answers

### Bear point 1 — "This is not a dislocation, it is a de-rating off a parabola, and the sell-off came *through* a beat."

**The strongest version:** implied price was $121.53 at 2025-12-31 (33.2x FY26 EPS). By
2026-07-02 an insider sold at $285.30 (78x trailing FY26 EPS). Then, across the July 28
beat-and-raise, the stock went to $191.59 by 2026-08-04. The drawdown is not the market
misreading a bad quarter — the quarter was good and guidance was up 25% — so the market was
correcting a valuation, and it has corrected it only back to 38.5x forward against a 20.7x
five-year median. There is nothing left to arbitrage; the *remaining* 18 turns are still the
overvaluation, not the opportunity.

**My answer: I cannot rebut this, and it is the reason my own math fails.** The three
supporting facts are all primary-source and all mine, not the bear's — the Form 4 price
series, the cover-page multiple history, and the 8-K guidance that the market ignored. The
most I can say in mitigation is that at $185.60 the price is *below* the $188.93 the company
itself paid in the June quarter and below every Form 4 sale price since 2026-08-04 except
one. That is a floor argument, not a return argument. **Conceded.**

### Bear point 2 — "35.6% net margin is peak, because it is volume-driven and the volume is a WFE cycle."

**The strongest version:** the company's own gross-margin bridge says the FY26 improvement was
"revenue volume of products and services +0.5pt" and "manufacturing labor, overhead and
efficiencies +0.2pt", with mix **negative** 0.2pt. Volume-driven margin unwinds when volume
unwinds. The 10-K says the business "has historically been cyclical with respect to these
manufacturers' capital equipment procurement practices." A 20% revenue decline at 64.6%
incremental gross margin and roughly fixed R&D takes operating margin from 41.7% to roughly
33% and net margin toward 28% — precisely the FY24 level — cutting EPS to ~$2.90 and, at
20.7x, a price near $60.

**My partial answer.** Three things are true and disclosed. (a) The FY24 comparison the bear
uses is depressed by a $289.5M goodwill/intangible impairment and FY25 by $239.1M; ex-those,
the net-margin series is 31.0% `[EST]` / 35.4% / 35.6%, i.e. the level was reached in FY25 and
has held, not spiked. (b) The trough is cushioned by the 23% of revenue that is service, which
is a function of *installed tools and utilization*, not of new-tool orders — and installed base
only grows. (c) Fixed-cost risk is low: net debt is $985.0M and the earliest maturity is
March 2029, so a downturn does not create a financing problem. **What I cannot answer:** I
cannot show the trough net margin, because KLA does not disclose service gross margin, so I
cannot compute how much of the 61.3% gross margin survives a product-revenue decline. The
bear's ~28% trough estimate is as defensible as any I could produce. **Partially conceded.**

### Bear point 3 — "China at 30% of revenue and one customer at 19% are two rising, uncontrollable exposures."

**The strongest version:** 29.8% of FY26 revenue ships to China, into a regime the 10-K
describes as an expanding list of BIS rules and Entity List additions, with the explicit
warning: "We may lose revenue in future periods related to anticipated sales to customers in
China unless we are able to replace their orders." Simultaneously, a single customer went from
13% of revenue (FY24) to **19% (FY26 and FY25)**. Concentration risk is rising on both axes at
once, and the second is six points from the §4 kill line.

**My answer, which is genuine.** China exposure is falling, not rising: **42.8% → 33.3% →
29.8%** across FY24–FY26, and the 10-K attributes flat FY26 China revenue to legacy-node demand
offsetting export-control losses on advanced transactions — i.e. the controlled portion has
*already been substantially removed* and the business grew 11.7% anyway. The remaining China
revenue is disproportionately legacy-node and service, the parts the controls do not reach.
That is a de-risking, and it is the one structural change in this file that argues for a higher
terminal multiple. On the customer: 19% is below the 25% kill threshold, and the counterparty is
TSMC, disclosed by name [10-K FY26 MD&A], whose capex is the single most-forecast number in the
industry. But it is moving 3 points a year and P5 exists for exactly this reason. **Answered on
China; monitored on the customer.**

### Bear point 4 — "Gate 5 and Gate 6 both fail, so the compounder multiple is unearned."

**The strongest version:** insiders own 0.09%; bonus runs on an adjusted operating-margin
metric that excludes goodwill impairment (which the company took in each of FY24 and FY25);
PRSUs run on non-GAAP EPS. Capex is 10% of FCF and capital returns are 89%. By the fund's own
rules this is a capital-return story, underwritten at a FCF yield — and the FCF yield is 1.55%.

**My answer: I concede the gate scores and dispute only the framing.** The gate-6 test is
"≥50% of FCF redeployable at ≥ current ROIC" — KLA's current ROIC is 66.5%, an almost
impossible reinvestment bar, and the company grew revenue 38.4% over two years while spending
only $375.9M of capex. The growth is coming from R&D ($1,532.1M, 11.3% of revenue), which the
gate framework treats as opex, not reinvestment. That is a real limitation of the gate as
applied to an asset-light process-control franchise inside a Type B column. **But the
conclusion is the same either way**: a business that returns 89% of FCF is valued off its FCF
yield, and 1.55% is not a yield that supports a 15% IRR. On gate 5 I have no defence — the comp
metrics are adjusted, on the face of the proxy, and the ownership is 0.09%.

## 6. What would change my mind

Ordered by how much each would move the IRR.

1. **A multiple re-anchoring.** If the price falls to **$148** (28x exit) or **$110** (20.7x
   median exit), the same bull operating path clears 15%. Nothing about the business needs to
   change; only the entry. This is the single cleanest way this becomes a buy, and it is the
