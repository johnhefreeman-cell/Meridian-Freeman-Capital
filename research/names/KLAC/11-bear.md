# KLAC — Bear Case

**KLA Corporation · CIK 0000319201 · Type B (Asset-heavy operating) · FYE 30 June**
Prepared 2026-09-05. All figures from `00-base-facts.md` or the tool calls cited.
Fiscal ≠ calendar: FY26 = 2025-07-01 → 2026-06-30.

---

## Conclusion first

The 40% drawdown is **not** the market pricing a WFE downcycle and it is **not**
structural business impairment. It is a **spike unwinding, and it has not
finished unwinding.** KLAC at $185.60 still carries a market capitalization
**52.2% above** where it stood eight months ago and **189.7% above** where it
stood twenty months ago, against revenue that grew 11.7% and net income 18.9%
over the same span. The business is fine — management guided the September
quarter **up 24.6% YoY** on 28 July 2026 and beat its own midpoint in each of
the last three quarters. The price is what is wrong.

**The primary bear mechanism is multiple compression, not impairment.** That
matters for sizing, because compression from 50.7x trailing GAAP EPS toward
KLA's own recent history of 23.7–34.5x is a **-34% base case and a -64% bear
case**. Both breach the CLAUDE.md §5 limit of a bear that loses <25%. The
business-impairment bear exists (China 29.8%, one customer 19.0%, $5.97bn of
purchase commitments against a backlog the company says is cancellable) but it
is the *second* risk, not the first.

roughly double it. §5 requires the terminal multiple to be at or below the
trailing 5-year median unless we can name the structural change. We can name a
candidate (rising process-control intensity per wafer with advanced packaging
and leading-edge AI logic) but we cannot yet prove it from a filing, so the
median governs.

### 1.4 Management's own revealed price opinion

Q4 FY26 monthly repurchases [10-K FY26 Item 5]: April 1,184,390 sh @ **$170.47**;
May 999,790 sh @ **$178.64**; June 836,720 sh @ **$227.35**. Today's $185.60
sits between the April and May prints.

More telling: **KLA bought 39% fewer shares in FY26 (18.2m) than FY25 (30.1m)
for 6% more dollars.** As the price ran, the board bought fewer shares. That is
the operator's revealed view of value, and it is the single cleanest
capital-allocation data point in the file.

Everything below is real and cited, but none of it is the reason the stock fell
40%, and none of it individually breaks the earnings power. It is the tail that

## 2. Revenue quality

### 2.1 KLA is not a recurring-revenue business, and Type B gate 1 does not clear

| FY26 revenue | $m | % |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wafer Inspection | 6,630.8 | 49% |
| Patterning | 2,706.8 | 20% |
| Specialty Semiconductor Process | 502.5 | 4% |
| PCB & Component Inspection | 460.3 | 3% |
| **Services** | **3,125.9** | **23%** |
| Other (refurb / upgrades) | 153.1 | 1% |
| Total | 13,579.5 | 100% |

Source: 10-K FY26 Note 17.

**Repeat revenue is 24% (services + refurb/upgrade), against a Type B gate 1
bar of ≥50% repeat.** The alternate test — aftermarket attach ≥30% of gross
profit — cannot be scored because KLA does not disclose service gross margin.
Even at the corporate 61.3% margin, service gross profit would be $1,916m on
total gross profit of $8,324.4m = **23%**, below 30%. At a more typical service
margin the figure is nearer 15%. **Gate 1: FAIL on the disclosed test, UNKNOWN
on the alternate.**

76% of revenue is one-time system sales into customer capex budgets. The
"subscription-like" framing management uses for services [10-K FY26 MD&A]
applies to less than a quarter of the business.

### 2.2 Contracted revenue is thin and the long-dated piece is shrinking

Total deferred revenue is **13.1% of one year's revenue**, and only $238.1m

The tell is in the direction:

| | FY26 | FY25 | Δ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deferred system revenue (current) | 932.9 | 816.8 | +14.2% |
| Deferred service revenue (current) | 604.1 | 548.0 | +10.2% |

**Service revenue grew 16.5% while the total service contract liability fell
6.1% and the long-dated portion fell 31.7%.** The installed-base annuity is
being recognized faster than it is being rebooked at long duration. The cash
flow statement corroborates: deferred service revenue was a **-$54.6m** use of
cash in FY26 versus +$100.5m in FY25 [10-K FY26 statement of cash flows]. This
is not fraud; it is the mechanical signature of a service book whose forward
contracted duration is contracting while the current-period print grows.

### 2.3 Backlog is not a contract, and KLA says so

| | Jun-24 | Jun-25 | Jun-26 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Backlog | $9.83bn | $7.86bn | **$12.57bn** |

Source: 10-K FY25 and FY26, Item 1 "Backlog".

Backlog **fell 20% in FY25** then **rose 60% in FY26**. Revenue moved +23.9%
then +11.7%. Backlog does not track revenue. KLA states plainly:

> "Our backlog on any particular date does not provide meaningful information
> about the timing of future revenue recognition." [10-K FY26 Item 1]

and flags "increased risk of order modifications, **pushouts or
cancellations**." The FY25 decline was explained as suppliers catching up and
lead times normalizing — i.e. backlog is as much a lead-time artifact as a
demand signal. **A 60% backlog build is not underwritable as 60% of forward
revenue.** The bull will lean on it; it does not bear the weight.

[10-K FY26 Note 17]. TSMC is the only >10% customer named for FY24–FY26 [10-K
FY26 Item 1]. Below the §4 kill threshold of 25% — but the trajectory is
13% → 19% → 19% and the §4 line is six points away.

**China contributed $5.8m — 0.4% — of $1,423.3m of FY26 growth.** All of the

That is the impairment bear in one line: **the entire FY26 growth rate is one
capex theme, concentrated in a handful of fabs, one of which is 19% of the
company.** If AI infrastructure capex digests for a year, the growth engine
does not slow — it stops, and China (29.8% of the base, structurally capped by
export controls) cannot backfill it.

### 2.5 Receivables and factoring — the divergence

| | FY26 | FY25 | FY24 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | 13,579.5 | 12,156.2 | 9,812.2 |
| Accounts receivable, net | 2,889.2 | 2,263.9 | ~1,896.0 `[EST]` |
| **DSO (days)** | **77.7** | **68.0** | **70.5** `[EST]` |
| **Receivables sold under factoring** | **515.5** | **230.6** | **254.9** |
| Proceeds from sales of LC | 86.0 | 55.5 | 22.2 |
| Bad debt charged to expense | **28.4** | 11.5 | 5.9 |
| Write-offs (deductions) | **31.4** | 10.3 | 6.8 |
| Allowance / AR | **1.07%** | 1.50% | 1.73% |

Source: 10-K FY26 balance sheet, MD&A "Factoring Arrangements", Schedule II.
FY24 AR is `[EST]`, reconstructed as FY25 AR less the FY25 cash-flow AR change
of $367.9m; label it estimated.

Three things move the wrong way at once:

1. **Revenue +11.7%, AR +27.6%.** DSO up 9.7 days.
2. **Factoring more than doubled**, +$284.9m (+123.6%). Add the incremental
   factoring back and FY26 DSO is **85.3 days** — up ~17 days like-for-like.
   The company is buying cash-flow timing from banks; FY25 and FY24 factoring
   were flat to each other, so this is a FY26 step-change, not a policy KLA has
   always run at this scale.
3. **Bad debt expense rose 2.5x and actual write-offs rose 3.0x, while the
   allowance ratio fell 43bps** (1.50% → 1.07%). Charging less reserve per
   dollar of receivable while writing off three times as much is the wrong
   direction. In dollars it is small — roughly $12m of pre-tax benefit versus
   holding the FY25 rate — but the sign matters.

KLA carries a dedicated risk factor: "We are exposed to risks related to our
receivables factoring and banking arrangements… If we were to stop entering
into these factoring arrangements, our operating results, financial condition
and cash flows could be adversely impacted by delays or failures in collecting
trade receivables" [10-K FY26 Item 1A]. That is the company telling you the
cash conversion depends on the facility.

### 2.6 What I checked and did *not* find

- **No related-party revenue** disclosed. Kill not tripped.
- **No percentage-of-completion.** Revenue is point-in-time on transfer of
  control [10-K FY26 Note 1]. Payment terms are 70–90% within 30–60 days of
  shipment [10-K FY26 Note 2] — tight, which makes the DSO extension more
  notable, not less.
- **No acquisitions in FY26** (business acquisitions net of cash: $0 in FY26,
  $0 in FY25, $3.7m in FY24) [statement of cash flows]. **All 11.7% growth is
  organic.** This is genuinely disconfirming for the accounting-aggression
  bear.
- **PwC issued a clean opinion and flagged Revenue Recognition as the critical
  audit matter**, testing occurrence of product and service revenue and
  subsequent cash receipts on outstanding invoices [10-K FY26, report of
  independent registered public accounting firm]. The auditor looked exactly
  where I looked and signed.

**Gate 3: PASS on the 3-year average (0.93), FAIL in FY26 standalone (0.78).**

cash flow — which is why the FCF bear is a timing bear, not a permanent one.

### 3.1 Buybacks are more anti-dilution than return of capital

Shares outstanding 1,320,227k (Jun-25) → 1,306,983k (Jun-26): **-13.2m net**,
while 18.2m shares were repurchased [10-K FY26 balance sheet and Note 10]. So
~5.0m shares of gross issuance from RSUs and ESPP were absorbed. At the FY26
average repurchase price of $126.31 that is **$631.6m — 27.6% of the $2,289.8m
buyback — spent purely to stand still.**

SBC expense was $310.2m [statement of cash flows]. The **cash** cost of
maintaining the share count was 2.0x the expense line. True FCF after holding
share count flat: 3,767.1 − 631.6 = **$3,135.5m**, a **1.29% yield** on today's
market cap.

SBC as a share of FCF is 8.2% — not abusive on its own. The point is the
GAAP expense understates the economics by half.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goodwill & intangible impairment | **0** | 239.1 | 289.5 |
| Acquisition-related charges (non-GAAP add-back) | 178.1 | 219.7 | — |

### 4.1 The one balance-sheet item that is a real downside lever

**Purchase commitments: $5.97bn at 2026-06-30, "a majority of which will be due
within the next 12 months"** [10-K FY26 MD&A]. Prior year: **$2.42bn** [10-K
FY25 MD&A]. **+146.7% in one year, against 11.7% revenue growth.**

Management explains it: "We have also increased our purchase commitments, in
part to secure the supply of key components, which may affect the timing and
magnitude of our costs and working capital requirements" [10-K FY26 MD&A].

Size it: $5.97bn is **44.0% of FY26 revenue** and **113.6% of FY26 cost of
revenues**, contracted, majority within twelve months, against a $12.57bn
backlog the company itself says is subject to "pushouts or cancellations." Add
inventory already on the books at $3,648.5m (253 days of COGS, up 6.7 days YoY).

**This is the mechanism by which a demand air-pocket becomes an earnings
air-pocket.** If AI capex digests in CY2027, KLA takes delivery of components
it has committed to, inventory days blow out, and the lower-of-cost-or-NRV test
in Note 1 produces write-downs charged to cost of revenues. KLA carries the
risk factor explicitly: "We have recorded significant asset impairment,
restructuring and inventory write-off charges and may do so again in the
future" [10-K FY26 Item 1A]. This is the single largest quantified downside
lever in the filing and it is the reason my bear-case gross margin goes below
the FY24 trough.

## 5. Disconfirming disclosures found

I looked hardest for removed disclosure. **I found none.** What I found instead
cuts against the impairment bear, and it is reported here without softening.

**5.1 Management guided the September quarter UP 24.6% YoY.** [8-K
2026-07-28, acc. 0000319201-26-000024, Ex-99.1]
- Q1 FY27 revenue **$4.0bn ± $200m** vs. Q1 FY26 actual $3,209.7m = **+24.6%**.
- GAAP gross margin **61.6% ± 1.0%** vs. Q4 FY26 actual 61.4%.
- GAAP diluted EPS **$1.14 ± $0.10** vs. $1.04 in Q4 FY26.
- CEO Wallace: momentum "**accelerating in the second half of calendar 2026 and
  continuing through 2027**."

There is no guide-down. The 40% drawdown happened *against* an accelerating
guide. That is dispositive: this is a multiple event.

### 6.1 The finding that drives sizing

**The bear case loses 64.1%. The §5 limit is a bear that loses <25%. It is
breached by 39 percentage points.**

Worse — and this is the number that should govern the position:

**The compression-only bear, with the business performing exactly to the base
case and zero impairment, still loses 33.6%.** There is no version of this
where the asymmetry works, because the loss is not contingent on anything going
wrong operationally. It is contingent only on the multiple returning to KLA's
own recent history.

**And the bull case returns 10.2% p.a. — below the §5 base-case hurdle of 15%.**
When the *bull* misses the hurdle, the trade is not a compounder entry at any
size.

### 6.2 Break-evens — what today's price requires you to believe

- **To break even over three years** on base FY29 EPS of $4.90, the exit
  multiple must be **37.9x** — above every observation in either primary-source
  anchor series (max observed: 34.5x, FY26).
- **To earn the §5 hurdle of 15% p.a.**, the exit multiple must be **56.9x** —
  a permanent 57x on a cyclical capital-equipment maker.
- **To justify $185.60 at the median 24.5x**, FY29 EPS must be **$7.58**, i.e.
  net income of ~$9,399m on ~1,240m shares, i.e. revenue of **$26.4bn at a
  35.6% net margin — a 25.0% revenue CAGR for three straight years.** KLA's
  actual FY23→FY26 revenue CAGR was **8.9%**; FY21→FY24 was **12.4%**.
  Twenty-five percent compounded for three years is roughly double anything in
  the record.

## 8. What would change my mind

Ranked by how much each would move the bear.

1. **A disclosed multi-year contracted revenue figure, or service gross margin.**
   If KLA discloses that service gross profit is ≥30% of total gross profit,
   gate 1 flips and the "77% of revenue is cyclical system sales" framing
   weakens materially. *Watch: FY27 10-K Note 17, or any IR disclosure of
   service segment margin.*

2. **Two more quarters at the guided pace with DSO reverting.** If Q1 and Q2
   FY27 land at or above $4.0bn **and** AR grows slower than revenue **and**
   factoring falls back toward the $230–255m FY24–FY25 level, the revenue-quality
   bear is dead and only the compression bear remains. *Watch: 10-Q for Sep-26
   (due ~Oct-26) and Dec-26 (due ~Jan-27) — AR line and the MD&A factoring table.*

3. **Purchase commitments falling back toward $2–3bn.** The jump to $5.97bn is
   the largest quantified downside lever in the file. If it reverses without an
   inventory write-down, my bear-case gross margin of 57.5% is too harsh.
   *Watch: FY27 10-K MD&A "Material Cash Requirements".*

4. **China revenue growing again with licenses granted.** China contributed
   0.4% of FY26 growth and is 29.8% of the base. If it returns to growth, the
   single-theme concentration risk halves. *Watch: 10-Q geographic disclosure;
   any 8-K on BIS licensing; the Affiliates Rule at its November 2026 expiry.*

5. **The board buying aggressively at these prices.** The FY26 pattern was
   fewer shares for more dollars. If FY27 Q1/Q2 repurchases show a step-up in
   *share count* at prices near $185, the operator disagrees with me and I would
   weight that heavily — they have $9.74bn of authorization. *Watch: Item 5 of
   each 10-Q.*

6. **Insider open-market purchases.** Zero code-P transactions in six months
   against $84.16M of sales. A single meaningful purchase by Wallace or Higgins
   would be the loudest possible contradiction. *Watch: Form 4s.*

7. **A structural argument for >34.5x that can be tied to a filing.** If KLA
   discloses process-control intensity per wafer rising — dollar content per
   leading-edge wafer start, or advanced-packaging attach rates — the §5
   exception for exceeding the trailing median becomes nameable. Today it is
   not, and that is why the base case uses 24.5x.

**What would *not* change my mind:** another quarter of backlog growth. KLA has
told us in writing that backlog "does not provide meaningful information about
the timing of future revenue recognition," and the FY25 20% decline followed by
the FY26 60% increase proves it.
