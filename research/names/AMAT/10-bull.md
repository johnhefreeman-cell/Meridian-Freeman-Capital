**Applied Materials, Inc. · CIK 0000006951 · Type B (asset-heavy operating) ·
FYE late October · Price $454.71 (base facts, 2026-09-05)**

---

## Read this first

I was asked to prove the drawdown is cyclical rather than structural and that
the asymmetry is real. **I can prove the first. I cannot prove the second, and
two hard stops fire before the valuation debate even matters.**

1. **§4 universal kill criterion is triggered.** $173.2M of open-market insider
   sales (transaction code S) in the trailing six months, zero purchases, and
   **not one of the eleven Form 4s carries a Rule 10b5-1 plan disclosure**
   (`aff10b5One = 0` on every filing; no plan referenced in any footnote). CEO
   Gary Dickerson alone sold $104.8M on 2026-06-15 and 2026-06-29 at $590–$736,
   within days of the $739.67 52-week high. §4 says these are not weighed
   against positives. See "The kill" below for the one mitigating fact.
2. **Gate 5 fails on the letter and is non-negotiable.** All directors and
   executive officers as a group hold 2,405,861 shares = **0.30%** of
   792,705,219 shares outstanding [DEF 14A 2026-01-28, p. beneficial-ownership
   table], versus the ≥3% bar. And PSU vesting keys off "non-GAAP economic
   profit, relative TSR, non-GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP operating margin"
   [DEF 14A 2026-01-28, CD&A]. CLAUDE.md §3.1: *"Comp tied to 'adjusted'
   anything is a gate 5 failure regardless of type."*
3. **Gate 1 fails on the Type B aftermarket test.** AGS is 14.6% of 9M FY26
   gross profit against a ≥30% bar.
4. **The math does not clear §5 even in the bull case.** Bull 3-yr IRR 14.7%
   at a *contracting* exit multiple. Base case 2.4%. Bear loses 41.5% against a
   §5 limit of 25%.

Everything below is the strongest honest version of the positive case, so the
bear cannot claim it was never built. The business analysis is genuinely good.
The price is not.

## 1. Mechanism — one sentence

**Applied's aftermarket franchise (Applied Global Services) has become an
earnings floor that does not fall when the equipment cycle falls, and it is now
large enough — $7.1bn of year-end backlog, larger than the equipment segment's
own backlog for the first time in the disclosed series — that a WFE downcycle
now hits a structurally higher-margin, structurally more contracted business
than the one that de-rated in 2019.**

Edge type: **analytical** — the segment note and the five-year backlog series
carry the counter-cyclicality proof, and it is not published as a single
number anywhere.

I chose this mechanism over "leading-edge intensity rising per wafer" because
intensity-per-wafer is not disclosed in any AMAT filing. It is a sell-side
construct. Under §7 I will not build a mechanism on a number I cannot cite.

### 2d. Where the AGS claim fails

Honest accounting of what AGS does *not* do:

- **It does not clear Type B gate 1.** Aftermarket attach must be ≥30% of gross
  profit. AGS is **14.6%** of 9M FY26 gross profit. The alternative limb —
  "≥50% repeat revenue" — has **no supporting disclosure**: AGS is 21% of 9M
  FY26 revenue and AMAT publishes no repeat-revenue metric for Semiconductor
  Systems. **Gate 1 = FAIL.** This is a finding, not a rounding issue: AGS is
  an earnings *floor*, not an earnings *majority*.
- **AMAT discloses no installed-base count, no renewal rate, and no attach
  rate.** The FY2020 10-K disclosed "over 44,900 installed … systems"; the FY25
  10-K dropped it. Remaining performance obligations on contracts with original
  duration ≥1 year are only **$1.7bn** [FY25 10-K Note 7], because AMAT elects
  the practical expedient to exclude sub-12-month contracts and "the majority of
  revenue recognized over time is recognized within 12 months of contract
  inception" [10-Q 3Q26 Note 14]. **The subscription-model claim in the MD&A is
  not quantified anywhere in the filings.** Anyone underwriting AGS as a
  software-like annuity is underwriting an undisclosed number.
- AGS gross margin is **34.9%**, not 50%+. It dilutes consolidated gross margin.
  Its value is stability, not mix.

### 3d. §5 compliance — stated plainly

- **Multiple expansion as a share of return: 0%.** Every case above exits at a
  multiple *below* the 39.1x entry multiple on clean FY26E EPS. I did not test
  the trailing 5-year median multiple because there is no price series
  available — the market MCP is down and no price file exists in the repo.
  **Trailing 5-year median multiple: UNVERIFIED.** §5 says the terminal
  multiple must be at or below that median unless a structural change is named,
  so with the median unverifiable I held the exit multiple below entry in all
  three cases rather than assert a structural re-rating I cannot evidence.
- **Base-case IRR 2.4% versus the §5 hurdle of ≥15%. FAIL.** To reach a 15%
  base-case IRR on base-case FY29 EPS of $15.57 you need a $670.5 exit price =
  **43.1x**, ten percent *above* the entry multiple. At that point essentially
  the entire excess return over the earnings-only path is multiple expansion —
  a §5 breach and, in the language of the rule, a bet on sentiment.
- **Bear case loses 41.5% versus the §5 limit of <25%. FAIL.**
- The asymmetry is inverted: bull +50.7%, bear -41.5%.

## 6. Steelmanned bear points, and my answers

### Bear point 1 — "China export controls are a structural revenue cliff, not a cycle."

**The bear's strongest form:** China was 37% of revenue in FY24. The U.S.
government has repeatedly tightened; AMAT has been subpoenaed by DOJ, BIS and
SEC since 2022; AMAT paid **$253M** to BIS in February 2026 and accepted a
**suspended denial order** that only gets waived three years out and only if
audits are completed on time [10-Q 3Q26 Note 13]. A denial order, if
un-suspended, is an existential export-privileges event. New rules cut $549M
off FY24 backlog and $989M off FY22 backlog [FY24 10-K, FY22 10-K].

**My answer — this one I can answer, with the geography table.**

| China revenue | FY24 | FY25 | 9M FY26 | Q3 FY26 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $ | 10,117 | 8,529 | 6,688 | 2,506 |
| % of total | 37% | 30% | 28% | 28% |
| YoY | — | **-16%** | +2% | **-2%** |

Source: FY25 10-K geographic table; 10-Q 3Q26 geographic table.

The de-rate already happened and the company grew through it. China revenue
fell 16% in FY25 and consolidated revenue still rose 4%. In Q3 FY26, China
revenue fell 2% YoY while consolidated revenue rose 25% — meaning **non-China
revenue grew 39.0% YoY** (6,609 vs 4,754, derived from the geographic table).
The US grew 100% and Europe 202% in the quarter. The bull case does not need
China; it needs China not to go to zero, and the earnings base has already
been rebuilt without it.

What I cannot answer: the suspended denial order is a genuine tail. If the
audit requirements are not met, the consequence is not modellable and not
hedgeable. I do not underwrite it; I monitor it (P9).

### Bear point 2 — "You are underwriting the top of the WFE cycle at 39x earnings."

**The bear's strongest form:** Revenue is +25% YoY, guided +51% YoY for Q4,
gross margin is at an all-time high in the 13th consecutive quarter of
expansion, memory is inflecting (DRAM 26% of Semiconductor Systems revenue vs
22% a year ago), and the tax rate is at 12.9%. Every one of those is a
peak-cycle input. Applying a 32x multiple to FY29 EPS built on 36% operating
margins is capitalising a peak on a peak. The FY19 precedent says revenue can
fall 13% and operating income 25% in a single year.

**My answer is partial, and I will say where it stops.**

What I can answer: the *shape* of a downcycle has changed. In FY19 AGS was
$3.9bn of revenue and 32.9% of trough operating income; today AGS is running
$6.7bn annualised, with backlog 1.12x its own revenue and 48% of company
backlog. Consolidated gross margin is 660bps above the FY19 trough level and
the company has $7,957M of net cash with one $1.2bn maturity inside 24 months.
A repeat of FY19 in percentage terms lands on a much better balance sheet and
a much better margin structure.

What I cannot answer: **the multiple.** At $454.71 the stock is at 31.8x
EV/FY26E EBIT and a **1.64% FCF yield**. The "39% below the high" framing in
the base facts is a comparison to $739.67, which was itself roughly 19x
revenue. I have no price history to compute a trailing 5-year median multiple —
the market MCP is down — so I cannot establish that today's multiple is below
its own history. **I concede this point: the bear is right that the entry
multiple, not the business, is what breaks the case.** My own base case earns
2.4% a year precisely because of it. This is the point that ends the thesis,
and I would rather state it here than have it found at convergence.

### Bear point 3 (I raise it because the bear will) — "Management is selling and comp is on non-GAAP."

I cannot answer this. See §7.

## 9. What would change my mind

I would reopen this name if **all three** of the following became true:

1. **Price.** Entry EV/NTM EBIT ≤20x — on base-case FY27 operating income of
   $13,366M that is EV $267,320M, equity $275,277M, **≈$348/share**. On the
   cross-check, a ≥3.5% FCF yield on FY27E FCF of ~$7,000M `[EST]` implies
   **≈$253/share**. Call the reopen band **$250–$350**. At today's 31.8x and
   1.64% there is no version of this model that clears a 15% base-case IRR
   without multiple expansion, and §5 forbids that.
2. **Insiders.** Two consecutive quarters with either no code-S sales above
   $10M in aggregate, or Form 4s that disclose a plan adoption date. A single
   open-market purchase by Dickerson or Hill would carry more weight than any
   quarter of results.
3. **Comp.** A proxy in which PSU vesting is tied to GAAP or per-share metrics
   rather than non-GAAP economic profit and non-GAAP operating margin.

I would kill it permanently, rather than park it, if:

- AGS backlog falls below $6,000M in the FY26 or FY27 10-K (the mechanism is
  gone);
- the BIS suspended denial order is un-suspended;
- 1Q FY27 revenue prints below $8,500M, which would mean the Q4 FY26 guide was
  a pull-forward rather than a run-rate.
