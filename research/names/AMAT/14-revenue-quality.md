# AMAT — Revenue Quality

**Applied Materials, Inc. · CIK 0000006951 · Type B (asset-heavy operating), as assigned in `00-base-facts.md`.**
FY ends last Sunday in October; FY26 Q3 = 2026-04-27 → 2026-07-26. USD millions.

## Conclusion first

The revenue is real. The **durability is not proven, and the Type B gate 1 test fails on
the one limb that filings let us measure.** Applied Global Services is 22.5% of revenue but
only **15.5% of consolidated gross profit** (FY25) — half the 30% aftermarket-attach
threshold — and AMAT discloses no repeat-revenue percentage, no renewal rate and no
installed-base count in any filing. Gate 1 = **FAIL** on the measurable limb, **UNKNOWN**
on the other. Gate 3 = **PASS** on the FY23–FY25 three-year average (0.99) but the TTM
figure has broken down to **0.61** on a capex step-up.

The customer-concentration kill does **not** trip: largest customer 19% FY25, 20% 9M FY26,
both under the 25% line. But there is **no multi-year contract** — AMAT's own revenue note
says the contracts are "signed purchase orders, master agreements, and sales orders," and
its backlog note says orders are cancellable. Concentration is 6 points of one customer's
capex budget away from a kill, with nothing contractual underneath it.

**A separate, universal kill criterion does trip: $173.2M of open-market insider selling in
the trailing six months with `aff10b5One = 0` on all 24 Form 4s.** See §6.

### 1.2 Contract term — the answer is "there isn't one"

This is the part that matters more than the percentage, and it is unambiguous in the filing.

- **What counts as a contract:** "We generally consider written documentation including, but
  not limited to, **signed purchase orders, master agreements, and sales orders** as
  contracts." [10-K FY25, Note 1 Revenue Recognition]
- **Remaining performance obligations on contracts with original duration of one year or
  more: $1.7bn** as of 2025-10-26, of which ~53% recognized within 12 months. That is **6.0%
  of FY25 revenue.** Everything else is short-duration and excluded under the practical
  expedient. [10-K FY25, Note 7]
- **RPO note describes those contracts as "primarily consisting of written purchase orders
  received from customers."** Not master supply agreements with volume commitments.
- **Cancellability:** "Backlog is subject to change, including the addition of new orders,
  potential amendments or cancellations of existing orders... Customers may delay delivery of
  products or cancel orders prior to shipment, subject to possible cancellation penalties."
  [10-K FY25, Item 1, Backlog]
- Risk factor: "Our products are configured to customer specifications, and changing,
  rescheduling or canceling orders may result in significant, non-recoverable costs."
  [10-K FY25, Item 1A]

**Verdict: there is no multi-year contract with the 19% or 15% customer disclosed anywhere
in the filings.** The relationship is purchase-order-based and cancellable with penalties.
The kill does not trip only because the percentage is 19–20%, not because a contract protects
it. If a single customer's share crosses 25% in any fiscal year, §4 fires immediately and
there is no contract to appeal to.

### 1.5 Export control — a policy risk, not a cyclical one

- "Since 2022, we have received **multiple subpoenas** from government authorities requesting
  information relating to certain China customer shipments and export controls compliance,
  including from the **U.S. Department of Justice, the U.S. Commerce Department Bureau of
  Industry and Security, and the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.** We also have
  received subpoenas from the U.S. Department of Justice requesting information related to
  certain federal award applications... **we cannot predict the outcome, nor reasonably
  estimate a range of loss or penalties.**" [10-K FY25, Note 14 Commitments and Contingencies]
- Export regulations "have limited the market for certain of our products and services,
  **adversely impacted our revenues** and increased our exposure to foreign and Chinese
  domestic competition." [10-K FY25, Item 1A]
- Backlog is explicitly exposed: subject to change from "changes in export rules and
  regulations." [10-K FY25, Item 1, Backlog]

28–30% of revenue sits in a jurisdiction where a rule change is a step-function, not a cycle.
There is an open, unquantified enforcement exposure. This is **not** a "bad quarter vs. broken
business" call of the kind §1 says we have edge on — it is a policy binary, and §1 puts
theses that depend on policy outcomes out of mandate. It does not by itself kill the name, but
any bear case that relies on "the cycle turns" is mis-specified: the downside distribution here
has a policy tail that no cash-flow inflection cures.

## 3. Durability evidence — gate 1, Type B column

**Test (CLAUDE.md §3.1, Type B): ≥50% repeat, or aftermarket attach ≥30% of gross profit.**

### 3.1 Limb 2 — aftermarket attach as a share of gross profit: FAIL

| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 9M FY25 | 9M FY26 | 3Q FY26 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AGS revenue | 5,732 | 6,225 | 6,385 | 4,236 | 5,005 | 1,781 |
| AGS gross profit | 1,821 | 2,137 | 2,134 | 1,407 | 1,748 | 634 |
| Consolidated gross profit | 12,384 | 12,897 | 13,808 | 10,543 | 11,968 | 4,586 |
| **AGS % of gross profit** | **14.7%** | **16.6%** | **15.5%** | **13.3%** | **14.6%** | **13.8%** |
| AGS % of revenue | 21.6% | 22.9% | 22.5% | 19.6% | 20.8% | 19.5% |
| AGS gross margin | 31.8% | 34.3% | 33.4% | 33.2% | 34.9% | 35.6% |

Source row: [10-K FY25, Note 15 segment tables] for FY23–FY25; [10-Q 3Q26, Note 14 segment
tables, acc. 0001628280-26-058235] for FY26 and recast FY25 interim periods. FY26 periods
exclude 200mm equipment, which moved to Semiconductor Systems effective 1Q FY26.

**Threshold 30%. Actual 13.3%–16.6% across four years. FAIL, and not marginally — it is
roughly half.** The reason is structural: AGS carries a **33–36% gross margin against
Semiconductor Systems' 52–55%**, so AGS's revenue share (~22%) mechanically compresses to a
~15% gross-profit share. AMAT's aftermarket is large in dollars and *low-margin* relative to
the equipment it attaches to. That is the opposite of the classic industrial razor/razorblade
shape the Type B gate was written to detect.

Cross-check on operating income (the alternative reading): AGS segment operating income as a
share of the two reportable segments' combined operating income = 18.2% (FY23), 20.6% (FY24),
19.5% (FY25), 19.1% (9M FY26). **Also fails 30%, on every basis we can construct.**

**This answers the base-facts hypothesis directly: AGS does not clear the aftermarket test.**
The taxonomy is not vindicated here. Semicap is not the same animal as an industrial with a
service annuity — AMAT's service business is real but it is a low-margin logistics-and-labour
business bolted to a high-margin equipment business, not a profit annuity.

### 3.2 Limb 1 — ≥50% repeat revenue: UNKNOWN

AMAT discloses **no repeat-revenue percentage, no renewal rate, no net revenue retention, no
installed-base unit count, and no subscription-share-of-AGS figure** in the FY25 10-K or any
FY26 10-Q. The qualitative statements are directional only:

- "Our strategy is to continue to shift the AGS' service and spares business to a
  **subscription agreement model**... providing us a more predictable revenue stream."
  [10-K FY25, Item 7] — a strategy statement with no metric attached.
- "Demand for services is expected to grow as our installed base of systems and chambers
  increases and **customers renew long-term service agreements.**" [10-K FY25, Item 7] — no
  renewal rate given.
- Grep of the FY25 10-K for "renewal rate" and for any installed-base count returns nothing.

AMAT publishes a service-agreement renewal rate in investor materials. **Per the standing
rule, a deck figure with no filing equivalent cannot be used to pass gate 1, and it is not
used here.**

What the filings *do* give, and what it is worth:

| Evidence | Value | Reads as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backlog, total | 19,011 (FY22) → 17,171 (FY23) → 15,873 (FY24) → **15,002 (FY25)** | Falling three years running while revenue rose |
| Backlog, AGS | 5,643 → 5,162 → 6,767 → **7,141 (48% of total)** | AGS backlog is the only piece growing; 1.12× AGS FY25 revenue |
| Backlog, Semi Systems | 12,691 → 11,127 → 8,259 → **7,105** | Down 44% in three years |
| Backlog / revenue cover | 0.65× (FY23) → 0.58× (FY24) → **0.53× (FY25)** | Forward visibility shrinking |
| Implied book-to-bill [EST] | FY24 0.95, FY25 0.97 | Two straight years shipping out of backlog |
| RPO ≥1yr original duration | **1,700** = 6.0% of FY25 revenue | Genuinely contracted revenue is thin |
| Contract liabilities | 2,975 (FY23) → 2,849 (FY24) → **2,566 (FY25)** = 9.0% of revenue | Deferred revenue *shrank* 9.9% in FY25 while revenue grew 4.4% |

Source row: [10-K FY25 Item 1 Backlog; 10-K FY24 Item 1 Backlog; 10-K FY23 Item 1 Backlog;
10-K FY25 Note 7]. Book-to-bill is `[EST]` — method: revenue + change in stated backlog,
divided by revenue; AMAT does not publish book-to-bill.

The AGS backlog line is the strongest durability evidence in the filings: $7,141m, 48% of
total backlog, 1.12× AGS annual revenue, and growing 38% over two years while equipment
backlog fell 44%. That is consistent with a widening service annuity. But backlog is
explicitly cancellable and is a management-defined Item 1 metric, not an audited RPO — and
the audited RPO is $1.7bn. We can say the service base is growing. We **cannot** say from a
filing that ≥50% of revenue is repeat.

### 3.3 Gate 1 verdict

| Limb | Threshold | Measured | Score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aftermarket attach % of gross profit | ≥30% | **15.5% (FY25), 14.6% (9M FY26)** | **FAIL** |
| Repeat revenue | ≥50% | Not disclosed in any filing | **UNKNOWN** |

**Gate 1 = FAIL.** One limb is measurable and fails by a factor of two; the other is not
establishable from primary sources and is therefore UNKNOWN, which per §3.1 is not a pass.
Open work item: obtain a filing-grade repeat-revenue or renewal-rate disclosure. Absent one,
this gate cannot be upgraded.

### 4.1 What the table says

**Cash conversion by year:**

| Period | Net income | OCF | Capex | FCF | FCF/NI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY23 | 6,856 | 8,700 | 1,106 | 7,594 | **1.11** |

| FY25 | 6,998 | 7,958 | 2,260 | 5,698 | **0.81** |
| **FY23–FY25 3-yr avg** | 21,031 | 25,335 | 4,556 | 20,779 | **0.99** |

| 12-quarter aggregate | 23,549 | 23,763 | 5,752 | 18,011 | **0.77** |

**Gate 3 (Type B: FCF/NI ≥0.8, 3-yr avg) = PASS at 0.99 on FY23–FY25.** That is the test as
written, and it passes cleanly.

**But the trend is the story, and it is deteriorating fast.** FY25 barely cleared at 0.81;
TTM through 3Q26 is 0.61; 2Q FY26 converted **7 cents of FCF per dollar of net income**. Two
drivers, and they are different in character:

1. **Capex step-up — a discrete build, not a working-capital problem.** Capex went
   1,106 → 1,190 → 2,260 (FY25) → 2,773 TTM, a 2.3× rise in two years on 4% revenue growth.
   It is not maintenance capex. In 9M FY26, **$1,563m of the $1,988m total sits in "Other"**
   — the unallocated corporate line — versus $367m in Semiconductor Systems and $58m in AGS
   [10-Q 3Q26, Note 14]. Segment capex is 1.8% of segment revenue. FY25 capex was "primarily
   for investments in **real property and improvements**, demonstration and testing equipment,
   manufacturing and network equipment" [10-K FY25, Item 7]. This is a facilities program that
   should end; it depresses FCF/NI without indicating anything about revenue quality. This is
   also the §5 valuation caveat for Type B: maintenance capex is far below D&A, so EV/EBITDA
   is not distorted the way the "maintenance capex >60% of D&A" rule guards against — the
   distortion runs the other way.

2. **Receivables building faster than revenue in 3Q26 — watch this.** AR rose from 5,772 to
   **7,691 YoY, +33.2%, against revenue +24.8%.** Ending DSO went 66.7 (3Q24) → 71.9 (3Q25) →
   **76.8 (3Q26)**, a 10-day extension over two years. On 3Q26 revenue, 10 days of DSO is
   ~$1.0bn of cash. It is not yet a red flag — 76.8 days is unremarkable for capital equipment
   with acceptance-gated final payments — but it is the second consecutive year of extension
   and the first quarter where AR growth clearly outran revenue growth.

**What is *not* wrong, and matters:**

- **Contract liabilities grew with revenue in 3Q26**: 2,470 → 3,271, **+32.4%** versus revenue
  +24.8%. Customers are paying deposits ahead of a bigger book. Deferred revenue growing
  *faster* than revenue is the opposite of the classic pull-forward tell. This is the single
  best cash-quality signal in the table.
- **Contract assets are shrinking**: 306 → 227 YoY, and 281 → 227 since FY25 year-end.
  Revenue recognised ahead of billing is *falling* while revenue grows 25%. There is no
  unbilled-receivable build. If AMAT were pulling revenue forward on acceptance-gated deals,
  this line would be inflating. It is not.
- **12-quarter cumulative OCF/NI = 1.01.** Over three years, every dollar of accounting profit
  became a dollar of operating cash. The gap between 1.01 and 0.77 FCF/NI is entirely capex.

### 4.2 Two adjustments the reader needs

**(a) Net income is inflated by non-operating income in FY26, which makes FCF/NI look worse
than the operating business deserves.** Interest and other income, net: **+566 (1Q26), +771
(2Q26), −100 (3Q26) = +1,237 for 9M FY26**, versus +625 in 9M FY25 [10-Q 1Q26/2Q26/3Q26,
consolidated statements of operations]. That is **14.6% of 9M FY26 pre-tax income of $8,460m**
from a non-operating line. The effective tax rate also collapsed: 12.9% for 9M FY26 versus
27.2% for 9M FY25. Stripping the non-operating income at the reported rate lifts TTM FCF/NI
from 0.61 to roughly **0.69 `[EST]`** — still below 0.8, still capex-driven. The point stands
either way, but **reported net income growth in FY26 is materially non-operating and should
not be read as revenue quality.**

**(b) AMAT sells receivables.** $501m (FY25), $444m (FY24), $679m (FY23), generally without
recourse [10-K FY25, Note 6]. This flatters both DSO and OCF by roughly 5–6 days and ~$0.5bn
respectively. The FY26 10-Qs do not restate the figure, so the 9M FY26 amount is **UNVERIFIED**
— an open item. If the FY26 pace matches FY25, the true DSO at 3Q26 is ~82 days, not 76.8.

## 5. Is the rising gross margin real or an amortisation artifact?

**Real. It is not an amortisation roll-off.** The MRVL trap does not apply here.

| Test | Evidence | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is purchased-intangible amortisation rolling off? | Purchased technology and other intangible assets, **net: $315m at 2026-07-26 vs $226m at 2025-10-26** [10-Q 3Q26, balance sheet]. The balance **went up 39%**. | **No.** An amortisation roll-off requires a declining intangible balance. AMAT's is rising. |
| Is amortisation material to COGS at all? | Total D&A across all segments was $151m in 3Q26 [10-Q 3Q26, Note 14] — that is PP&E depreciation *plus* intangibles, against COGS of $4,529m. Intangible amortisation is a small fraction of 3.3% of COGS. | **Immaterial.** Even a full roll-off could not move GM by 130bps. |
| Is it segment mix? | Semiconductor Systems GM **53.4% → 55.3%** YoY; AGS GM **33.8% → 35.6%** YoY [10-Q 3Q26, Note 14]. SS share of revenue moved 76.2% → 77.2%. | **Both segments expanded.** Mix contributed ~20bps of the 150bps; the rest is genuine segment margin. |

| What does management attribute it to? | "Gross margin... increased primarily driven by **higher revenue, increases in average selling prices, and lower material and manufacturing costs.** The increase... in the three months ended July 26, 2026 was also driven by favorable changes in product mix." [10-Q 3Q26, MD&A] | Volume leverage + price + cost, on a 24.8% revenue increase. Consistent with the segment data. |

**Conclusion: the 49.0% → 50.3% progression over three quarters is operating leverage, ASP and
mix, not an accounting artifact.** The genuine caveat is that it is **cyclical** — ASP and
absorption gains at a 24.8% revenue growth rate reverse symmetrically at a −24.8% one. The
margin is real; its durability at this level is a function of where WFE spending sits, and the
segment-level evidence in §3 says the high-margin half of the business is the *cyclical* half.

**Gate 1 fails and cannot be repaired from public filings.** It fails for a specific and
non-obvious reason: AMAT's aftermarket is *large but low-margin*, so a 22.5% revenue share
becomes a 15.5% gross-profit share. The base-facts hypothesis — that AGS would clear the
aftermarket test and vindicate the Type B taxonomy for semicap — is **not supported.** The
gate can only be moved by a filing-grade repeat-revenue or renewal-rate disclosure that
AMAT does not currently provide.

**Gate 3 passes on the three-year average the rule specifies, but the reader should not stop
there.** The pass is carried by FY23 (1.11) and FY24 (1.04); FY25 scraped through at 0.81 and
the trailing twelve months sit at 0.61. The cause is a discrete facilities capex program —
$1,563m of $1,988m of 9M FY26 capex is unallocated corporate, not segment capex — and OCF/NI
over the same twelve quarters is 1.01, so the operating business converts profit to cash
one-for-one. If the build program rolls off on schedule, gate 3 stays a genuine pass. If capex
stays above $2.5bn while revenue mean-reverts, it does not. **Falsifying signal: FY27 capex
guidance above $2.5bn, or a fourth consecutive quarter of AR growing faster than revenue.**
