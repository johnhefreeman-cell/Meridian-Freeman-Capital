## Gate 1 also fails — and my Type B hypothesis was wrong

The base-facts file predicted AMAT would be a clean **Type B** fit, on the
theory that semicap's installed-base service business is the aftermarket
annuity Type B gate 1 tests for. **That is not supported by the filings.**

| | Aftermarket attach, % of gross profit | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FY22–FY25 range | **13.3 – 16.6%** | 30% |
| FY25 | 15.5% | 30% |
| 9M FY26 | 14.6% | 30% |

Applied Global Services is **22.5% of revenue** but carries a **33–36% gross
margin** against Semiconductor Systems' 52–55%. Its revenue share compresses to
roughly a **15% profit share**. Same answer on operating income, 19.5%.

**Semicap's aftermarket is large but low-margin — it is not a profit annuity.**
The repeat-revenue limb is `UNKNOWN`: no renewal rate, no installed-base count,
no repeat percentage in any filing. The ~90% renewal figure that circulates
exists only in investor decks and is not filing-tier evidence.

The best filing support is AGS backlog of $7,141M — 48% of total backlog and
1.12× AGS revenue, growing while equipment backlog fell 44% in three years.
Real, but cancellable, and not proof of ≥50% repeat.

**So the taxonomy question raised by MRVL is not resolved by AMAT.** Type B does
not vindicate here either.

## Customer concentration: not tripped, but the contract limb is empty

Largest customer **19%** (FY25) / **20%** (9M FY26); top two **34%**. Below the
25% threshold, so §4 does not trip on the percentage.

But the contract half of the criterion has nothing behind it. AMAT's own note
defines contracts as "signed purchase orders, master agreements, and sales
orders"; RPO with original duration ≥1 year is **$1.7bn, 6.0% of revenue**; and
backlog is explicitly cancellable.

**And the counterparties were de-identified.** "Samsung" and "Taiwan
Semiconductor" appear **zero times** in the FY25 10-K and the 3Q26 10-Q, against
five times in FY24. The percentages still tie to a filing; the names no longer
do.

That is the second semiconductor name in two workups to remove customer
identification disclosure while concentration stayed high.
