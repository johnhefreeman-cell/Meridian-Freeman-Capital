# AMAT — Bear Case

**Applied Materials, Inc. · CIK 0000006951 · Type B (asset-heavy operating), tested with
Type D valuation discipline · Prepared 2026-09-05 · Price $454.71 [00-base-facts.md]**

---

## Conclusion first

The 39% drawdown is not the risk. The remaining 61% is.

AMAT's business is performing. Revenue, gross margin and operating margin are all genuinely
better, and the gross-margin rise is **not** the MRVL amortisation trap. The bear case here
is almost entirely **multiple compression off a 2.5x re-rating that happened in sixteen
months**, compounded by a second, smaller **impairment bear** in the tax line and the
order book.

Three findings drive sizing:

1. **§5 asymmetry fails, and it fails in the base case, not the bear.** Holding a
   primary-source-anchored 18.6x exit multiple, the base case loses **38.2%** over three
   years (IRR −14.8%). The bear loses **74.4%**. There is no parameterisation at $454.71
   that produces a bear loss under 25%.
2. **A universal kill criterion is tripped.** $173.3M of net insider selling in the
   trailing six months, zero open-market purchases, and the Rule 10b5-1 checkbox
   (`aff10b5One`) reads **0 on every one of the 24 Forms 3/4** filed since 2026-03-05.
   The CEO sold $55.6M on 29–30 June at $700–736, days from the $739.67 52-week high.
3. **Gate 1 fails on the filed segment data.** The base-facts hypothesis was that AGS
   would clear the Type B aftermarket test. It does not. AGS is **14.6% of 9M FY26 gross
   profit** (15.5% FY25, 16.6% FY24) against a ≥30% threshold, and 20.8% of revenue
   against a ≥50% repeat threshold. AMAT is a ~79% capital-equipment company with a
   four-month systems backlog.

## 1. Compression bear vs. impairment bear

These are different risks and they size differently. Both are live here; the first is far
larger.

Two filed, primary-source market-value observations bracket the re-rating:

**Today AMAT trades at 35.7x FY26E non-GAAP diluted EPS of $12.75** (9M actual $8.73 +

$10,250M at the Q3 GAAP operating margin of 34.0%, plus D&A of ~$573M; EV $352,900M].

§5 requires the terminal multiple to be **at or below the trailing 5-year median**. On
every primary anchor available, that is 15–22x. The compression from 35.7x to 18.6x is
−48% before a single dollar of earnings changes.

### 1b. The impairment bear — smaller, but real and disclosure-grounded

Two mechanisms permanently lower earnings power below what the FY26 print implies:

**(i) The tax rate is a temporary, expiring, conditional incentive.**
GAAP effective rate 9M FY26 **12.9%**, versus 27.2% in 9M FY25 [10-Q, Note 12]. The
non-GAAP effective rate in Q3 FY26 was **11.0%** [8-K 2026-08-13]. AMAT's own disclosure
explains why this cannot hold:

> "The currently enacted legislation is expected to **materially increase our foreign taxes
> beginning in the first quarter of fiscal 2026**, primarily due to the implementation of
> the global minimum tax regime in Singapore." … "We have been granted additional
> **conditional** reduced tax rates in Singapore that **expire beginning in fiscal 2030**."
> [10-Q Q3 FY26, MD&A "Other"]

The 9M FY26 rate reduction was driven by "a remeasurement of deferred tax assets resulting
from new tax incentive agreements in Singapore" — a one-time DTA remeasurement, not a run-rate.
Normalising the 9M FY26 tax rate from 12.9% to 21% removes **$685M** of net income
(8,460 × 8.1pp), or 9.3% of the 9M print.

**(ii) GAAP net income is inflated by non-operating equity marks.**
9M FY26 "interest and other income, net" was **$1,237M**, of which **$927M** was net
gain on equity investments — $1,157M of *unrealised* gains on publicly traded equity
securities, less marks [10-Q, Note 3]. Reconcile the growth:

| 9M FY26 vs 9M FY25 | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | 24,037 | 21,568 | +11.4% |
| Operating income | 7,429 | 6,577 | **+13.0%** |
| Net income (GAAP) | 7,370 | 5,101 | **+44.5%** |
| Non-GAAP net income | **6,980** | 5,875 | +18.8% |

Source: 10-Q Q3 FY26 [0001628280-26-058235]; 8-K 2026-08-13 non-GAAP reconciliation.

Operations grew 13.0%. GAAP net income grew 44.5%. The 31-point gap is equity marks and
tax. **AMAT's own non-GAAP net income is $390M *lower* than GAAP** — the company itself
does not claim the GAAP number. Any model built on the 30.7% 9M net margin in the base
facts is modelling a mark-to-market gain and a Singapore tax ruling.

## 2. Revenue quality

### The order book is thin and the disclosure has thinned with it

- **Backlog is disclosed once a year, in the 10-K, and the last observation is
  ten months stale.** FY25 year-end backlog: **$15,002M** total — Semiconductor Systems
  **$7,105M** (47%), AGS $7,141M (48%), Other $756M. "Approximately 31% is not reasonably
  expected to be filled within the next 12 months." [FY25 10-K, Item 1, "Backlog"]
- Against 9M FY26 Semiconductor Systems revenue of $18,146M — a $24.2B annualised run
  rate — the $7,105M systems backlog is **roughly four months of coverage.**
- The ASC 606 disclosure is thinner still: remaining unsatisfied performance obligations
  on contracts with an original duration of one year or more are **approximately $1.0
  billion**, of which 73% recognises within 12 months [10-Q, Note 7]. AMAT elects the
  practical expedient for contracts of one year or less, which is to say **essentially
  the entire order book is under twelve months and cancellable.** The 10-K adds:
  "Customers may delay delivery of products or cancel orders prior to shipment."
- **There is no filing-level backlog figure for FY26 at all.** At the exact moment the
  question is whether Q4 FY26's $10,250M guide is a peak, the primary record is silent.
  Anyone underwriting 2027 growth is relying on the CEO's sentence, not a disclosure.

Gate 1 (Type B: ≥50% repeat, or aftermarket attach ≥30% of gross profit) — **FAIL**:

| | FY24 | FY25 | 9M FY26 (recast) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AGS revenue | $6,225M | $6,385M | $5,005M |
| AGS % of revenue | 22.9% | 22.5% | **20.8%** |
| AGS gross profit | $2,137M | $2,134M | $1,748M |
| **AGS % of total gross profit** | **16.6%** | **15.5%** | **14.6%** |

Source: FY25 10-K Note 15 [0001628280-25-056742]; Q3 FY26 10-Q Note 14. Note the FY26
figures are on a recast basis — the 200mm equipment business moved out of AGS into
Semiconductor Systems in Q1 FY26 and corporate support costs are now fully allocated,
which mechanically shrinks AGS. Even on the pre-recast FY25 presentation, AGS is 15.5% of
gross profit, half the 30% threshold. **The base-facts hypothesis is refuted.**

### DSO divergence — modest, but real, and partly a factoring artefact

| | Q3 FY25 | FY25 year-end | Q3 FY26 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DSO (company-disclosed) | 72 days | 69 days | **77 days** |
| Accounts receivable, net | $5,772M | $5,185M | **$7,691M** |

Source: 10-Q Q3 FY26 MD&A and Note 14; FY25 10-K MD&A.

AR grew **33.2%** YoY against Q3 revenue growth of 24.8% — 1.34x faster. The 9M FY26 cash
flow statement shows an AR drag of **$2,508M**, versus $538M in 9M FY25. AMAT's stated
cause is important and cuts both ways:

> "The increase in days sales outstanding was primarily driven by higher down payment
> invoices for which revenue has not yet been recognized as of July 26, 2026 and **lower
> accounts receivable factoring** compared to the same period in the prior year."
> [10-Q Q3 FY26, MD&A, Liquidity]

Down-payment invoices are a *good* sign — customers are prepaying. But the factoring
admission is a genuine earnings-quality finding: **AMAT sold $501M of receivables in FY25
and $444M in FY24** [FY25 10-K, MD&A and Note], which flattered those years' operating
cash flow. The FY26 factoring amount is **not disclosed in the 10-Q**, so the cash-flow
comparison to prior years is not clean and cannot be made clean from the filings. Mark
that gap; do not assume it is small.

### Customer concentration — rising, at the highest disclosed level, not yet a kill

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY23 | 19% | 15% | 34% |

20% is below the §4 kill line of 25% from one customer. It is also the highest figure in

discloses no contract term. Its own risk factor: "the actions of even a single customer
have exposed and can further expose our business and operating results to greater
volatility." **NOT TRIPPED — 5 points of headroom, deteriorating.**

### Pull-forward evidence

The revenue ramp is vertical and the mix is shifting toward the lumpiest end market:

| | Q1 FY26 | Q2 FY26 | Q3 FY26 | Q4 FY26 guide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | $7,012M | $7,910M | $9,115M | $10,250M ±500 |
| DRAM % of SemiSys | — | — | 26% (9M: 29%) | — |

Source: 10-Qs; 8-K 2026-08-13.

Q4 annualises to **$41,000M** against FY25's $28,368M. Semiconductor Systems DRAM mix rose
from 25% (9M FY25) to 29% (9M FY26), and MD&A attributes the memory increase to "customer

to account for and recognize revenue" [FY25 10-K, Report of Independent Registered Public

## 3. Earnings quality

### The gross-margin rise is real. The MRVL trap does not apply here.

This was the assigned question and the answer is against the bear. State it plainly.

| | Q3 FY25 | Q3 FY26 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GAAP gross margin | 48.8% | 50.3% |
| **Non-GAAP gross margin** | 48.9% | **50.4%** |
| Acquisition-related amortisation in COGS | $7M | $11M |

Source: 8-K 2026-08-13 non-GAAP reconciliation [0001628280-26-056699].

The entire GAAP-to-non-GAAP gross profit bridge is **$11M on $4,586M** — 10 basis points.
Purchased technology and other intangible assets, net, total **$315M** on a $43,522M
balance sheet [10-Q]. There is no amortisation to roll off. The FY23→FY26 progression —
46.7% → 47.5% → 48.7% → 49.8% (9M FY26) — is a four-year trend, and MD&A attributes it to
"increases in average selling prices, and lower material and manufacturing costs."

Decomposing Q3's +150bp: holding prior-year segment margins constant and applying current
segment weights gives 48.95% versus the 48.8% actual — **mix contributed ~15bp; rate
contributed ~135bp** [EST — method: segment revenue weights and segment gross margins from
10-Q Note 14]. Roughly 90% of the improvement is price and cost, not mix. **The margin is
real. Do not build the bear on it.**

### FCF/NI — gate 3 passes on a three-year average by 0.2 points and is deteriorating fast

| | FY24 | FY25 | TTM (to Q3 FY26) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash from operations | $8,677M | $7,958M | $8,396M |
| Capital expenditures | $1,190M | $2,260M | $2,773M |
| **Free cash flow** | **$7,487M** | **$5,698M** | **$5,623M** |
| Net income | $7,177M | $6,998M | $9,267M |
| **FCF / NI** | **1.04** | **0.81** | **0.61** |
| 3-yr aggregate | | | **0.802** |

Source: XBRL as reported, accessions 0000006951-24-000044, 0001628280-25-056742,
0001628280-26-058235. TTM = FY25 + 9M FY26 − 9M FY25.

**Gate 3: PASS, at 0.802 against a 0.80 threshold.** That is not a pass anyone should take
comfort from. Two facts sit underneath it:

- **Free cash flow has fallen 24.9% from FY24 while revenue rose.** AMAT's own non-GAAP
  free cash flow for 9M FY26 was **$3,580M, versus $3,655M in 9M FY25 — down 2.1%** while
  revenue grew 11.4% and non-GAAP net income grew 18.8% [8-K 2026-08-13, non-GAAP FCF
  reconciliation]. That is the single cleanest earnings-quality statistic in the file, and
  it comes from the company's own preferred measure.
- Capex is now **2.3x FY24** and running at **4.8x D&A** (9M FY26: $1,988M capex vs $413M
  D&A). §5 forbids EV/EBITDA on a Type B name where maintenance capex exceeds 60% of D&A
  without adjusting. Here capex is 481% of D&A — EV/EBITDA is meaningless for AMAT right
  now and I have used EV/FCF and P/E instead.

### Where the capex is going — a gate 6 problem

| 9M FY26 | Semiconductor Systems | AGS | **Other** | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | $18,146M | $5,005M | **$886M** | $24,037M |
| Operating income | $6,176M | $1,461M | **$(208)M** | $7,429M |
| **Capital expenditures** | $367M | $58M | **$1,563M** | $1,988M |

Source: 10-Q Q3 FY26, Note 14.

**79% of capital expenditure is going into the "Other" segment**, which produces 3.7% of
revenue and a $208M operating loss (widened from $16M a year ago). This is the EPIC Center,
display, and the Singapore/US build-out. It may prove to be excellent capital allocation.
As of today it is $1.56B of nine-month spend earning a negative return, disclosed in a
bucket the CODM explicitly says is not a reportable segment. Gate 6 asks whether ≥50% of
FCF is redeployable at ≥ current ROIC; the redeployment that is actually happening is into
the lowest-return bucket AMAT reports.

This spending is also policy-subsidised in a way that will not repeat indefinitely:
"Capital expenditure related incentives reduced gross property, plant and equipment by
**$1.8 billion** as of July 26, 2026… we have recorded **$1.2 billion of investment tax
credits**… expected to be refunded" [10-Q, Note 8]. The CHIPS Act credit requires
construction to begin before 1 January 2027.

### Serial "one-time" charges — NOT tripped, and the non-GAAP is unusually clean at the opex line

9M FY26 operating-line non-GAAP add-backs: acquisition items $37M + deal costs $8M + legal
settlement $253M + restructuring $12M = **$310M**, against GAAP operating expenses of
$4,539M = **6.8%**. Q3 FY26 alone: $21M on $1,511M = **1.4%**. The §4 threshold is >15% in
3 of 4 quarters. **NOT TRIPPED.** AMAT's aggressive non-GAAP adjustments live below the
operating line (investment marks, tax items) — which is where the earnings-quality problem
sits, not in add-backs to opex.

### SBC

$545M for 9M FY26, **15.2% of the $3,580M of free cash flow generated**, and 2.3% of
revenue. Share count fell (794M basic 9M FY26 vs 807M 9M FY25, −1.6%). Not a dilution
problem. Note that tax withholding on vested equity consumed **$437M** of cash in 9M FY26
versus $210M prior — a 108% increase driven by the share price, and a real cash cost.

## 5. Disconfirming disclosures found

### 5a. The BIS settlement and the suspended denial order — the highest-signal item in the file

> "On February 11, 2026, we entered into a settlement agreement with the U.S. Commerce
> Department Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) to resolve its inquiry relating to
> certain China customer shipments and export controls compliance and agreed to pay BIS an
> amount of **$253 million** … The settlement agreement also includes a **denial order that
> is suspended and will be waived three years after the date of the order** … provided that
> we have timely completed the audit requirements."
> [10-Q Q3 FY26, Note 13, Legal Matters]

And the consequence, stated by AMAT in its own risk factors:

> "Our failure to comply with the terms of the settlement agreement could result in
> significant penalties, **including the loss of the suspension of the denial order which
> would prohibit us from exporting certain of our products outside of the United States.**"
> [10-Q Q3 FY26, Part II Item 1A]

**85% of Q3 FY26 revenue was from customers outside the United States** [10-Q, Item 1A].
This is a three-year probationary period, running to February 2029 — inside the fund's
2–4 year holding period — during which a compliance failure converts an export-control
issue into an existential revenue event. It is low-probability. It is not zero, it is
disclosed, and the underlying conduct already occurred once.

This disclosure did **not** exist in the FY25 10-K (filed 2025-12-12) — full-text search
confirms "denial order" appears only in the three FY26 10-Qs. It is new disclosure, added
during the fiscal year in which the stock tripled.

### 5b. China — the structural bear, and it is already partly realised

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 9M FY26 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| China revenue | $7,247M | $10,117M | $8,529M | $6,688M |
| % of total | 27.3% | 37.2% | 30.1% | 27.8% |
| Q3 only | — | — | $2,548M (35%) | **$2,506M (28%)** |

Source: FY25 10-K Note 15; Q3 FY26 10-Q Note 14 and 8-K 2026-08-13.

China peaked in FY24 at $10,117M and has fallen 15.7% since. In Q3 FY26 China revenue was
**down 2% YoY in absolute dollars while total revenue rose 25%.** Every dollar of AMAT's
growth is now coming from outside China.

This cuts two ways and the honest reading is mixed:
- **Against the bear:** the China cliff is substantially *already taken*. A further
  restriction removes a shrinking, non-growing 28% rather than a growing 37%.
- **For the bear:** $2,506M per quarter — an $10.0B annualised base — remains exposed to a
  policy decision AMAT cannot influence and does not quantify. **AMAT discloses no
  export-licence-dependent revenue figure anywhere in the 10-K or 10-Qs.** The geographic
  table is the only quantification available. A "further restriction" cannot be sized from
  the filings, which is itself the finding: this risk is unmodellable from the primary
  record.

Additional China vector, newly disclosed: "in 2025 the Chinese government implemented
export controls on the export of **rare earth minerals that are used in certain of our
products** and may implement additional controls in the future" [10-Q Q3 FY26, Item 1A].
That is a supply-side, not demand-side, China exposure and it is not in anyone's model.

### 5c. Segment recast during the up-cycle

Effective Q1 FY26 the 200mm equipment business moved **out of AGS into Semiconductor
Systems**, and corporate support costs became fully allocated to segments. Prior periods
were recast [10-Q Note 14]. The effect is to move a lower-growth equipment line out of the
"services" bucket. I find no evidence this was done to flatter anything — recast prior
periods are provided and AGS margins improved on both bases — but it does mean **the AGS
series in the FY25 10-K and the AGS series in the FY26 10-Qs are not the same business**,
and any AGS-based durability argument must state which basis it uses.

### 5d. Executive compensation is tied to non-GAAP measures

> "Non-GAAP EPS and non-GAAP gross margin are performance targets under our bonus plans."
> … PSUs vest 50% on a "three-year non-GAAP **economic profit** goal (beginning with the
> FY25–FY27 performance period; previously 3-year non-GAAP operating margin)" and 50% on
> relative TSR.
> [DEF 14A filed 2026-01-28, 0001193125-26-027307, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]

The 8-K adds that non-GAAP measures are used "as performance measures in its executive
compensation program." FY25 non-GAAP EPS was $9.42 against GAAP diluted EPS of $8.66 — an
8.8% gap that flows directly into bonus determination.

**CLAUDE.md §3.1: "Comp tied to 'adjusted' anything is a gate 5 failure regardless of
type." Gate 5: FAIL.** Compounding it: all current directors and executive officers as a
group beneficially owned **2,405,861 shares = 0.30%** of the 792,705,219 outstanding at
2025-12-31 — against a ≥3% threshold. The "named operator" limb (Dickerson, CEO since 2013)
is the only thing keeping gate 5 from failing on both counts, and see §7 for what he did
with his shares.

### 5e. What I looked for and did **not** find

Stating these matters as much as the findings:
- **No material weakness.** KPMG issued an unqualified opinion on both the financial
  statements and internal control over financial reporting for FY25. No changes to ICFR in
  Q3 FY26. No auditor change; KPMG throughout. **No restatement of revenue.**
- **No missed guidance.** FY26 has been three consecutive beats: Q1 guide $6,850M ±500 →
  actual $7,012M; Q2 guide $7,650M ±500 → actual $7,910M; Q3 guide $8,950M ±500 → actual
  $9,115M with non-GAAP EPS $3.50 against a $3.36 guide [8-Ks 0001628280-25-051998,
  -26-007661, -26-035071, -26-056699]. The guidance framework is intact and credible.
- **No related-party transactions** material to earnings disclosed.
- **No goodwill risk.** Goodwill $3,873M against $25,626M of equity — 15.1%, and the
  business is not acquisition-built. Cash paid for acquisitions 9M FY26: $262M. Acquisition
  accounting is not flattering organic growth here.
- **Management is explicitly guiding growth into 2027.** Dickerson, 2026-08-13: "Based on
  the increased demand visibility we are receiving from our customers, **we expect another
  strong growth year for Applied Materials in 2027.**" Hill: "we are making additional
  manufacturing capacity investments to support projected demand **through the end of the
  decade.**" This is the strongest evidence against the cycle-peak bear and it is on the
  record. It is also unverifiable from any filing — see §7.

## 8. What would change my mind

Ranked by how much each would move the bear.

1. **Price.** The bear case is 74% of the way about price. At **$230** the base case
   (18.6x on $14.72 of FY29 EPS plus dividends = $280.8) returns +22.1% over three years,
   a 6.9% IRR; at **$180** it returns +56% and a 16.0% IRR, clearing §5. Everything below
   this line is second-order relative to that. *Falsifying signal: the price.*
2. **A quarterly backlog disclosure showing Semiconductor Systems coverage above 8–9
   months.** The FY26 10-K (expected ~2026-12-11) will disclose year-end backlog. If
   Semiconductor Systems backlog exceeds roughly $16B — more than 2.2x the FY25 figure and
   ~8 months of the Q4 run rate — the pull-forward and cycle-peak legs collapse.
   *Falsifying signal: FY26 10-K, Item 1, "Backlog."*
3. **Insiders buying, or 10b5-1 plans appearing.** If subsequent Form 4s carry
   `aff10b5One = 1` with adoption dates predating the June 2026 sales, the character of the
   kill changes — though the criterion as written is measured at the trailing-six-month
   window and would still be tripped. Open-market purchases (code P) by Dickerson or Hill
   would be a genuine reversal. *Falsifying signal: Forms 4.*
4. **Free cash flow converting.** If Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 show FCF/net income back above
   0.8 with DSO returning toward 69–72 days and the "Other" segment capex rolling off, the
   working-capital and capital-allocation legs of the impairment bear close.
   *Falsifying signal: 10-Q cash flow statement and MD&A DSO line.*
5. **A disclosed tax-rate bridge showing a sustainable sub-18% rate past fiscal 2030.**
   AMAT adopts the expanded income-tax disclosure standard (Topic 740) in its FY26 annual
   reporting, which will require the effective-rate reconciliation by category and taxes
   paid by jurisdiction. If that shows the low rate is structural rather than a Singapore
   incentive with a stated expiry, base-case EPS rises ~6% and the impairment bear halves.
   *Falsifying signal: FY26 10-K income tax note.*
6. **China stabilising above $2.5B/quarter for four consecutive quarters with no new BIS
   action.** That would convert China from an unquantifiable policy risk to a stable base.
   Conversely, a new Entity List addition covering a top-two customer, or any BIS finding
   under the settlement's audit requirements, un-suspends the denial order and the analysis
   ends. *Falsifying signal: 10-Q Note 13 and geographic revenue table.*
7. **AGS reaching 25%+ of gross profit.** AGS operating margin has expanded from 26.3% to
   29.2% and revenue grew 18% in 9M FY26. If the installed-base annuity scales to a quarter
   of gross profit, Type B gate 1 becomes arguable and the durability discount narrows.
   *Falsifying signal: 10-Q Note 14 segment table.*
