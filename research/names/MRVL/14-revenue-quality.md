# MRVL — Revenue Quality

**Prepared 2026-09-05. All figures USD millions unless noted. FY ends ~31 Jan;
FY26 = year ended 2026-01-31.**

---

## Conclusion first

Marvell's revenue is real, collected, and cash-generative — and it is **not
recurring, not contracted, and not disclosed in a way that lets gate 1 be
scored as written**. Contracted revenue we can prove from the filings is
**$40.1M of deferred revenue, 0.5% of FY26 revenue** `[10-K FY26,
0001835632-26-000011, Note "Accrued liabilities"]`. There is no backlog
disclosure anywhere in the 10-K (zero occurrences of the word), no remaining
performance obligation disclosure (practical expedient elected), no net
revenue retention, and no repeat-revenue metric. The company states plainly
that it "typically sell[s] products pursuant to purchase orders rather than
long-term purchase commitments" and that customers "cancel or defer purchase
orders on short notice without incurring a significant penalty"
`[10-K FY26, Item 1A Risk Factors]`.

Against the **Type A** column that the base-facts file assigns as the working
assumption, **gate 1 is FAIL, not UNKNOWN** — we can affirmatively establish
that contracted revenue is nowhere near 70%. Against a Type B reading, gate 1
is **UNKNOWN** (repeat revenue is plausible from design-win economics but is
not disclosed in any quantified form).

**A §4 Types-A-and-B kill criterion is tripped as written**: Distributor A was
**37% of FY26 net revenue and 45% of H1 FY27 net revenue**, and the filings
disclose no multi-year contract — the opposite, they disclose cancellable
purchase orders.

**Gate 3 (FCF/NI ≥0.8, 3-yr avg) is not computable as specified** because GAAP
net income was negative in FY24 and FY25. On every restatement that makes the
ratio meaningful, it clears: FY26 FCF/NI on a clean (ex-divestiture-gain) base
is **1.61 [EST]**, and FCF exceeded net income in all three years. Score
**PASS with the arithmetic caveat stated**, not a clean PASS.

Distributor A has risen every single year for six years: 13 → 15 → 20 → 24 →
34 → 37 → 45%. There is no year of reversal. This is not noise around a
threshold; it is a trend.

**Distributors are 50% of total revenue in H1 FY27** (Direct $2,553.8 / 50%;
Distributors $2,603.3 / 50%) `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, Note 3]`, against 42%
in H1 FY26. Distributor A at 45% of *total* revenue is therefore roughly **90%
of all distributor revenue**. The distributor channel is effectively one
counterparty.

### 1.2 Contract term — the part that decides the kill

There is no multi-year contract. The filings say the opposite, in three places:

- "We typically sell products pursuant to purchase orders rather than long-term
  purchase commitments. Some of our customers have, and others may in the
  future, cancel or defer purchase orders on short notice without incurring a
  significant penalty." `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]`
- "a significant portion of our sales are made on a purchase order basis, which
  allows our customers to cancel, change or delay product purchase commitments
  with relatively short notice to us" `[10-K FY26, Item 1A risk summary]`
- "while many of our customers are subject to purchase orders or other
  agreements that do not allow for cancellation, there can be no assurance that
  these customers will honor these contract terms" `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]` —
  note this is unquantified and is immediately hedged.

The only multi-year contracting Marvell discloses runs the **other** direction:
supply-side. "We typically place firm orders with our suppliers up to 26 weeks
prior to the anticipated delivery... may make further supply commitments up to
52 weeks... we have entered into and expect to continue to enter into capacity
reservation arrangements with certain foundries and substrate partners"
`[10-K FY26, Item 1]`, with $278.8M of prepayments on supply capacity
reservation agreements sitting in other non-current assets at FY26 year-end
`[10-K FY26, Note "Other non-current assets"]`. Marvell is contractually
committed to its foundries; its customers are not contractually committed to
Marvell. That asymmetry is the working-capital risk in a downturn.

### 1.3 The one mitigation, and why it does not hold up

The FY24 10-K explained the composition of Distributor A: "Net revenue
attributable to Distributor A increased due to the four module makers whose
business activity increased in fiscal 2024 as they were involved in supporting
data center sales to hyperscale customers" `[10-K FY24,
0001835632-24-000009, Item 1]`. The FY23 10-K gave a similar explanation
("four large module makers [changed] from direct to distribution accounts")
`[10-K FY23, Item 1]`.

So Distributor A is a channel aggregating multiple module makers who in turn
serve hyperscalers — it is not one economic end customer.

**But that disclosure has not been repeated since the FY24 10-K.** Neither the
FY25 10-K, the FY26 10-K, nor any FY27 10-Q re-describes what sits behind
Distributor A, and Distributor A has nearly doubled as a share of revenue since
that description was written. We cannot tie the mitigation to a current filing.
Under §7 and the universal §4 criterion ("the thesis requires trusting a number
we cannot tie to a filing"), the mitigation is **not usable**.

## 2. Recognition policy and judgments

### 2.1 The policy

"Product revenue is recognized at a point in time when control of the asset is
transferred to the customer. Substantially all of the Company's revenue is
derived from product sales... the performance obligation is deemed to be the
delivery of the product and therefore, the revenue is generally recognized upon
shipment to customers, net of accruals for estimated sales returns and rebates"
`[10-K FY26, Note 2 "Revenue Recognition"]`.

For distributors: "Control passes to the distributor upon shipment, and terms
and payment by our distributors is not contingent on resale of the product.
Product revenue on sales made to distributors is recognized upon shipment, net
of estimated variable consideration" `[10-K FY26, MD&A Critical Accounting
Policies]`.

**No change versus the prior year.** The FY25 10-K revenue recognition note is
word-for-word identical to the FY26 note `[10-K FY25, 0001835632-25-000057,
Note 2 vs 10-K FY26, Note 2]`. No policy change, no change in estimate
disclosed.

### 2.2 What is and is not present

| Judgment | Present? | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Percentage-of-completion | **No** | 100% point-in-time on shipment |
| Principal vs. agent (gross/net) | **Not discussed** | Distributors are customers, not agents; sell-in model. No agent analysis is disclosed because none is required — but it means the gross/net question is never tested in the filing |
| Material rights | **No** | No options-for-additional-goods disclosure |
| Significant financing component | **No** | "relatively short collection terms" `[10-K FY26, Note 2]` |
| **Variable consideration** | **Yes — the material one** | See below |
| **Consideration payable to a customer** | **Yes — customer warrants** | See below |

### 2.3 Variable consideration — sell-in with a large estimate on top

Because Marvell books distributor revenue on shipment into the channel rather
than on sell-through, every dollar of the 50% of revenue that goes through
distributors is recognized net of a management estimate:

> "Variable consideration primarily consists of price discounts, price
> protection, rebates, and stock rotation programs and is estimated based on a
> portfolio approach using the expected value method derived from historical
> data, current economic conditions, and contractual terms. Actual variable
> consideration could differ from these estimates."
> `[10-K FY26, MD&A Critical Accounting Policies]`

Size of the estimate:

| | FY25 (2025-02-01) | FY26 (2026-01-31) | Q2 FY27 (2026-08-01) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Variable consideration estimate (accrued liab.) | 517.9 | **713.8** | **777.7** |
| as % of that period's revenue (TTM for Q2 FY27) | 9.0% | 8.7% | 8.2% |
| Prepaid ship and debits (other non-current assets) | 516.9 | 584.2 | n/d |

Source: `[10-K FY26, Note "Accrued liabilities" and "Other non-current
assets"]`; `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, Note "Accrued liabilities"]`; TTM
revenue $9,450.3 per base-facts file.

"Substantially all of the variable consideration estimate is comprised of the
ship and debit claims accrual" `[10-K FY26, Note "Accrued liabilities"]`. An
8-9% of-revenue accrual, built on a portfolio expected-value model, is the
single largest recognition judgment in the file. It is not a red flag on its
own — it is stable as a percentage of revenue across three periods, which is
what you want to see. But it is the line to watch if channel inventory builds:
a sell-in model plus a rising ship-and-debit accrual is how semiconductor
revenue gets pulled forward.

The 10-K discloses the direction of travel on channel inventory in passing:
the increase in prepaid expenses was "primarily driven by higher prepaid ship
and debits **due to higher inventory balances at distributors**"
`[10-K FY26, MD&A Liquidity]`. Channel inventory is rising. Marvell does not
disclose weeks of channel inventory.

### 2.4 Customer warrants — revenue reduced by equity issued to a customer

> "During fiscal 2025, the Company issued a warrant to a customer for the
> purchase of up to 4.2 million shares... at an exercise price of $87.77 per
> share... The Fiscal 2025 Warrant Shares vest primarily based on the
> customer's achievement of qualifying product revenue milestones and are
> recognized as a reduction to revenue as qualifying revenues are recognized
> during the vesting term. The grant date fair value... total fair value of
> $227.6 million... A total of 0.7 million Fiscal 2025 Warrant Shares were
> vested as of January 31, 2026."
>
> "During fiscal 2026, the Company issued a warrant to a customer for the
> purchase of up to 1.0 million shares... exercise price of $87.00... total
> fair value of $55.4 million... None of the Fiscal 2026 Warrant Shares have
> vested as of January 31, 2026."
> `[10-K FY26, Note 3 "Customer Warrants"]`

Two observations. First, this is consideration payable to a customer, so
reported net revenue is already after a non-cash contra-revenue charge —
correct accounting, but it means GAAP revenue understates gross billings by the
vested-warrant amount, and that gap grows as more warrant shares vest. Second,
and more important for this workup: **Marvell is paying equity to secure
revenue from a large customer.** That is a price of the concentration, and it
dilutes. $283.0M of combined grant-date fair value is committed against future
revenue milestones through the five-year vesting terms. The warrants are struck
at $87.77 and $87.00 against the current $223.55 mark (holder's workbook, per
base facts) — deeply in the money.

Marvell does not disclose which customer, or the revenue milestones.

### 2.5 Contract balances

- Deferred revenue: **$40.1M at FY26 year-end**, $22.1M at FY25 year-end,
  $63.7M at Q1 FY27 `[10-K FY26 and 10-Q 0001835632-26-000019, "Accrued
  liabilities"]`. At Q2 FY27 deferred revenue is **no longer broken out** — it
  fell into the "Other" line of accrued liabilities `[10-Q
  0001835632-26-000025]`. Not disclosed.
- Revenue recognized in FY26 from the opening deferred revenue balance: "**not
  material**" `[10-K FY26, Note 3]`.
- **Unbilled receivables / contract assets: none.** No contract-asset line
  appears on the balance sheet and no such XBRL tag exists in the filer's
  companyfacts. Consistent with a pure ship-and-bill model.
- **Remaining performance obligations: not disclosed.** "The Company has
  elected the practical expedient and does not disclose the value of unsatisfied
  performance obligations for contracts with an original expected duration of
  one year or less" `[10-K FY26, Note 3]`. Marvell is telling us its contracts
  are short-dated.
- Sales commissions expensed as incurred under the practical expedient because
  "the amortization period is typically one year or less" `[10-K FY26, Note 3]`
  — a second, independent statement that the contract life is under a year.

## 3. Durability evidence — and the business-type question

### 3.1 Scored against Type A (the base-facts working assumption)

Gate 1, Type A column: **≥70% recurring or contracted.**

| Evidence Type A asks for | What MRVL discloses |
| --- | --- |
| RPO | **Not disclosed** — practical expedient elected, contracts ≤1 yr `[10-K FY26, Note 3]` |
| Deferred revenue | **$40.1M = 0.5% of FY26 revenue** `[10-K FY26]` |
| Subscription share | **Zero.** "Substantially all of the Company's revenue is derived from product sales" `[10-K FY26, Note 2]`; "revenue from services has been insignificant" `[10-K FY26, MD&A]` |
| Net revenue retention | **Not disclosed, not applicable** |

**Gate 1 verdict vs Type A: FAIL.**

This is a FAIL, not an UNKNOWN, and the distinction matters. UNKNOWN would mean
we cannot tell. Here we can tell: the affirmative disclosures — 0.5% deferred
revenue, no RPO, no services, cancellable purchase orders, no backlog — are
sufficient to establish that contracted revenue is *far* below 70%. There is
nothing further to find.

### 3.2 Scored against Type B, for completeness

Gate 1, Type B column: **≥50% repeat, or aftermarket attach ≥30% of gross
profit.**

| Evidence Type B asks for | What MRVL discloses |
| --- | --- |
| Backlog | **Zero mentions of "backlog" in the FY26 10-K.** Not disclosed |
| Service/parts revenue split | **No aftermarket exists.** Service revenue "insignificant" |
| Installed base | **Not disclosed** |

Repeat revenue is *economically* likely — design wins create sockets that run
for a product's life, and the top-10 customer share has been 63-82% for four
years — but Marvell publishes no cohort, no socket count, no repeat-revenue
percentage. **Gate 1 verdict vs Type B: UNKNOWN.**

Type B is in any case wrong on its face: PP&E net is **$935.0M** against
$8,194.6M of FY26 revenue `[10-K FY26, Note "Property and equipment, net"]` —
11% of revenue. Fabrication is outsourced. This is not capital in the ground.

### 3.3 The durability that does exist, and how it is evidenced

The qualitative case is genuine and worth stating precisely, because it is the
reason this name is not simply a commodity chip seller:

> "If a customer's system designer initially chooses a competitor's product, it
> becomes significantly more difficult for us to sell our products for use in
> that system because changing suppliers can involve significant cost, time,
> effort and risk for our customers. Thus, our failure to win a competitive bid
> can result in our foregoing revenues from a given customer's product line for
> the life of that product."
> `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]`

> "it can take from six months to three years from the time our products are
> selected to commence commercial shipments"
> `[10-K FY26, Item 1A]`

> "We often maintain substantial inventories to meet short lead time orders for
> **multi-year product runs**."
> `[10-K FY26, Item 1]`

So: a socket, once won, is sticky for a product generation, and product runs
are multi-year. That is real durability. It is also **entirely unquantified**.
Marvell never says how many sockets it holds, what share of revenue comes from
products more than two years in production, or what its design-win win-rate is.
The word "backlog" does not appear. There is no disclosure from which a 70%
threshold — or a 50% threshold — could be computed by anyone outside the
company.

### 3.4 Does the taxonomy need a fifth shape? Yes.

**Plainly: yes, and MRVL is the case that proves it.**

The four types in §3.0 sort businesses by *where the durability lives*:
Type A puts it in a contract, Type B in an installed base and aftermarket, Type
C on a balance sheet, Type D in a reserve. A fabless semiconductor company has
durability that lives in **none of those four places**. It lives in a design
win — a socket inside somebody else's product, held for that product's life,
defended by the customer's switching cost rather than by any obligation the
customer has undertaken.

Forcing MRVL into Type A produces exactly the failure mode §10 warns about. The
Type A gates would score: gate 2 PASS (GM 53.1%, rising), gate 1 FAIL. The
FAIL is not telling us the business is weak; it is telling us we applied a
contract test to a business that does not sell contracts. Meanwhile the tests
that would actually discriminate a good fabless business from a bad one — is
the socket position widening or narrowing, what is the revenue concentration
in products past their second year of production, is design-win share rising in
the sockets that matter — are not asked by any column in the table.

Recommended shape of a **Type E · Design-win / socket** column, built only on
things a semiconductor filer actually produces:

| Gate | Proposed Type E test |
| --- | --- |
| 1 · Revenue durability | Top-10 customer revenue share **stable or falling** over 3 years, **and** the largest single counterparty ≤30%, **and** management discloses design-win-to-production lead time and product-run length. Absent a disclosed backlog or socket metric, gate 1 is UNKNOWN — never PASS. |
| 3 · Earnings quality | FCF/NI ≥0.8 **and** FCF less SBC ≥0.8× NI, because acquisition amortisation and stock comp systematically flatter fabless FCF/NI |

Under that column MRVL would score gate 1 **FAIL** anyway — top-10 share is
*rising* (63 → 72 → 81 → 82%) and the largest counterparty is 45%. But it would
fail for the right reason, and a genuinely diversified fabless name (a Microchip
or a Lattice) would not be mechanically failed for the crime of not selling
subscriptions.

Type E would also cover auto Tier-1 component suppliers and aerospace
component makers — anyone whose revenue is a socket rather than a contract or a
consumable.

**Recommendation to the doctrine owner: add Type E. Until it exists, MRVL
should be recorded as Type A with gate 1 FAIL and this file cited as the reason,
rather than have the FAIL quietly reinterpreted.**

consideration liability, charged to other expense in H1 FY27
`[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, statements of operations and Note 6]`.
⁵ Deferred revenue was not separately disclosed at 2026-08-01; it was
aggregated into "Other" within accrued liabilities `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025]`.

### 4.1 The tell: receivables are outrunning revenue

This is the finding the section exists to produce.

| | Revenue YoY | AR YoY | DSO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q4 FY25 → Q4 FY26 | 1,817.4 → 2,218.7, **+22.1%** | 1,028.4 → 2,186.6, **+112.6%** | 51.5 → 89.7 |
| Q2 FY26 → Q2 FY27 | 2,006.1 → 2,739.3, **+36.5%** | 1,451.7 → 2,218.4, **+52.8%** | 65.9 → 73.7 |

Receivables have grown roughly five times faster than revenue year-over-year at
the Q4 print and about 1.4 times faster at the most recent quarter. DSO rose 38
days from the FY25 trough to the FY26 year-end, and remains 22 days above that
trough. On the factoring-adjusted basis the gap is the same shape (56.6 → 94.1
→ 77.4).

Marvell's explanation for the Q4 spike is linearity: "The increase in accounts
receivable was primarily due to **higher sales in the last two months of fiscal
2026**" `[10-K FY26, MD&A Liquidity]`. That is a plausible and benign reading —
a back-end-loaded quarter, not a collection problem — and the Q1 FY27 partial
reversal (AR down to 1,871.7 on higher revenue, DSO back to 70.4) supports it.
Allowance for credit losses is $4.5M on $2,191.1M gross AR
`[10-K FY26, Note "Accounts receivable, net"]`, i.e. 0.2% — no credit stress.

But the Q4 FY25 trough of 51.5 days was itself flattered: $868.8M of
receivables were factored during FY25 against $735.5M in FY26 and a run-rate of
$412.0M in H1 FY27. The trough was low partly because more receivables were
sold. So the *true* deterioration is smaller than the 38-day headline and the
*true* base was never as good as 51.5 days. Both directions of that correction
should be applied.

Verdict on cash conversion: **collection is lagging revenue, modestly and
explicably, and it is worth a standing monitor rather than a kill.** The
falsifying signal is Q3/Q4 FY27: if DSO does not come back under ~70 days on a
factoring-adjusted basis while data-center revenue keeps compounding, the
linearity explanation stops working and the question becomes channel stuffing.

### 4.2 Receivables factoring — quantified, because it moves OCF

Marvell "sells certain of its trade accounts receivable on a non-recourse basis
to a third-party financial institution... accounts for these transactions as
sales of receivables and **presents cash proceeds as cash provided by operating
activities**" `[10-K FY26, Note "Accounts receivable, net"]`.

| Period | Receivables sold | Still subject to servicing at period end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FY24 | 335.7 | 155.9 |
| FY25 | 868.8 | 101.8 |
| FY26 | 735.5 | 108.5 |
| H1 FY26 | 526.8 | 142.0 |
| H1 FY27 | **412.0** | 111.1 |

Source: `[10-K FY26, Note "Accounts receivable, net"]`;
`[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, Note "Accounts Receivable, net"]`;
`[10-K FY24, 0001835632-24-000009]`.

This matters two ways. It inflates OCF relative to a no-factoring baseline by
accelerating collections into the period, and it deflates reported DSO. The
useful news is that the programme is **shrinking** — $412.0M in H1 FY27 against
$526.8M in H1 FY26 — so the recent OCF strength ($1,244.3M in H1 FY27, +56.6%
YoY) is achieved on *less* factoring, not more. Cash generation is getting
better on an underlying basis, not being manufactured. Factoring fees were "not
material."

### 4.3 Gate 3 — FCF/NI

**As specified (3-yr average, GAAP):**

| | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OCF | 1,370.5 | 1,681.2 | 1,750.5 |
| Capex | 336.3 | 284.6 | 354.1 |
| **FCF** | **1,034.2** | **1,396.6** | **1,396.4** |
| Net income | **(933.4)** | **(885.0)** | 2,670.1 |
| FCF/NI | nm (neg.) | nm (neg.) | 0.52 |

Source: `[10-K FY26, 0001835632-26-000011, consolidated statements of cash
flows and operations]`; FY24/FY25 net income also confirmed at
`[DEF 14A 0001104659-26-060253]`.

The three-year average is **not computable**. Two of three denominators are
negative, and the FY26 denominator contains a $1.8B one-time gain. Reporting
"−0.72" (the naive mean) or "4.49" (the aggregate) would both be nonsense.

**Restated to a base that means something:**

FY26 net income includes a **$1.8B pre-tax gain on the automotive ethernet
divestiture**, recorded in interest income and other, net `[10-K FY26, Note 1
and MD&A]`. This resolves the base-facts open item 2 — the Q3 FY26 spike to
$1,901.3M is the Infineon sale, not an operating result.

| Basis | FCF | NI | FCF/NI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY26 reported | 1,396.4 | 2,670.1 | 0.52 |
| FY26, NI less the $1.8B pre-tax gain **[EST]**¹ | 1,396.4 | **870.1** | **1.61** |
| TTM to Q2 FY27, reported | 1,730.1 | 2,639.9 | 0.66 |
| TTM to Q2 FY27, less the gain **[EST]**¹ | 1,730.1 | 839.9 | 2.06 |

¹ **[EST] method:** the full $1,800.0M pre-tax gain is deducted from net income
with **no offsetting tax adjustment**, because the 10-K does not isolate the tax
on the gain (it states only that the FY26 tax expense increase "was driven by an
increase in earnings, which includes the gain on the sale," at a 12.4% effective
rate on $376.5M of total expense `[10-K FY26, Note 12]`). Since the gain was
taxed, the true clean net income is **higher** than $870.1M and the true ratio
is **lower** than 1.61 — but bounded well above 0.8 on any reasonable tax
assumption (at a 12.4% rate on the gain, clean NI ≈ 1,093.3 and FCF/NI ≈ 1.28).

**Cross-check, FCF less stock-based compensation.** FCF exceeds net income here
largely because of non-cash charges that are economically real to shareholders:
amortisation of acquired intangibles ($942.0M in FY26, $1,052.6M FY25,
$1,097.9M FY24) and SBC ($590.8M FY26, $597.4M FY25, $609.8M FY24)
`[10-K FY26, consolidated statements of cash flows]`. SBC is dilution — diluted
share count rose 5.84% YoY `[base facts, 10-Q 0001835632-26-000025]`. So:

| | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FCF less SBC | 424.4 | 799.2 | **805.6** |
| ÷ clean NI (FY26 only) | — | — | **0.93** |

Even after charging SBC against free cash flow, FY26 clears the 0.8 bar.

**Gate 3 verdict: PASS, with the caveat that the 3-year average as literally
specified is undefined.** FCF was positive and large in all three years while
GAAP net income was negative in two — cash exceeded accounting earnings
throughout, which is the direction that supports earnings quality. Capex is
modest and stable at 3.5-5.2% of revenue, consistent with fabless.

Capex, for the record and to close base-facts open item 4:
FY24 **336.3** · FY25 **284.6** · FY26 **354.1** · H1 FY27 **282.4**
`[10-K FY26 and 10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, investing activities]`.
**H1 FY27 FCF = 1,244.3 − 282.4 = 961.9.**

## 5. Organic versus acquired

### 5.1 FY26 — reported +42.1% understates organic growth

**No acquisition closed during FY26.** The only portfolio event was a
**divestiture**: the automotive ethernet business sold to Infineon on
2025-08-14, at the start of Q3 FY26 `[10-K FY26, Note 1]`.

The transaction was **not** presented as discontinued operations, so the 10-K
discloses no revenue for the divested business. Marvell's deal press release
does: "This business in fiscal 2026 is **expected to generate revenue in the
range of $225-250 million**" `[8-K filed 2025-04-08, 0001193125-25-074816,
Ex-99.1]`.

**[EST] — organic FY26 growth. Method:** take the $237.5M midpoint of the
company's stated FY26 full-year expectation for the divested business. Marvell
owned it for the first two fiscal quarters (2025-02-02 to 2025-08-14, ~28 of 52
weeks), so FY26 includes roughly half a year: **~$118.8M**. For FY25, the
automotive/industrial end market totalled **$322.4M** `[10-K FY25,
0001835632-25-000057, Note 3]`; automotive ethernet is a subset of that
(the remainder being industrial ethernet switches, US military/government
solutions and video surveillance, which were retained `[10-K FY26, Item 1
product table and its divestiture footnote]`). Assume automotive ethernet was
**~$210M** of FY25, scaled back from the FY26 expectation. Both years restated
to exclude it entirely:

| | FY25 | FY26 | Growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reported | 5,767.3 | 8,194.6 | **+42.1%** |
| Less auto ethernet **[EST]** | (210.0) | (118.8) | |
| **Organic [EST]** | **5,557.3** | **8,075.8** | **+45.3%** |

The divestiture is a **~3.2 percentage-point drag** on reported FY26 growth.
Organic growth is *better* than the headline, not worse. Sensitivity: at the
$225M and $250M ends of the company's range the organic figure runs +45.1% to
+45.5% — the estimate is not sensitive to the assumption.

Marvell's own attribution of the increase: data center **+46%**, communications
and other **+31%**, "partially offset by a decrease in sales from our
automotive ethernet product portfolio due to the divestiture" `[10-K FY26,
MD&A]`. Consistent.

### 5.2 H1 FY27 — acquired contribution is disclosed as immaterial

Two acquisitions closed at the start of FY27:
- **Celestial AI**, closed 2026-02-02, total purchase consideration **$3.5B**
  (cash ~$1.3B, ~24.5M shares, plus $315.8M of contingent consideration)
  `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, Note 4]`.
- **XConn Technologies**, closed 2026-02-10, **$469.0M** `[10-Q
  0001835632-26-000025, Note 4]`.

Revenue contribution:
- "**Revenue and earnings of Celestial since the acquisition date were not
  material**" `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, Note 4]`.
- "Pro forma financial information, as well as the **revenue and earnings of
  XConn since the acquisition date, were not material** for the periods
  presented" `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, Note 4]`.

So **H1 FY27 revenue growth of +32.2% is organic**, on the company's own
statement, and understated by the auto ethernet divestiture in the base period.

**[EST] — organic H1 FY27 growth. Method:** H1 FY26 (ended 2025-08-02) still
contained the automotive ethernet business for the whole half; H1 FY27 contains
none of it. Removing the ~$118.8M estimated half-year contribution from the base:

| | H1 FY26 | H1 FY27 | Growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reported | 3,901.4 | 5,157.1 | **+32.2%** |
| **Organic [EST]** | **3,782.6** | **5,157.1** | **+36.3%** |

**Bottom line on organic: MRVL's growth is organic. There is no acquired
revenue to strip.** What the acquisitions *did* buy is technology and people at
a $4.0B combined price, financed with $2.8B of new goodwill (total goodwill
$13.9B at Q2 FY27, up from $11.1B) `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025, Note 5]`, ~24.5M
newly issued shares, and a contingent consideration liability that has already
cost $433.7M of pre-tax income in H1 FY27 through fair-value remeasurement
`[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025]`. That last item is the answer to base-facts open
item 3: **Q1 FY27 net income of $34.5M was crushed by the Celestial contingent
consideration mark, not by an operating deterioration.**

Note for whoever scores gate 5 and the universal kill list: the contingent
consideration remeasurement runs through GAAP net income and will keep doing so
through FY29 as the milestone shares are marked. It moves in the same direction
as the share price. Every dollar of stock appreciation makes GAAP earnings look
worse and will be added back in non-GAAP. That is a legitimate add-back, but it
is going to be a large and recurring one — check it against the "serial
one-time charges >15% of GAAP opex" criterion each quarter.

**On the concentration kill, read this before acting.** §4 says kills are not
weighed against positives. As written, this one trips: Marvell's own disclosure
labels Distributor A a "customer," it is 45% of revenue, and there is no
multi-year contract. The honest counter-argument is that a distributor is a
channel, not an economic end customer, and that the four module makers behind
it serve multiple hyperscalers — so end-customer concentration is lower than
45%. That counter-argument may well be right. It is also **not supported by any
filing published in the last two years**, and Marvell has never disclosed the
end-customer split behind the channel. The fund cannot verify it. My
recommendation is to log the kill, and to reopen the name only if Marvell
re-discloses the composition of Distributor A or the end-customer concentration
behind it.

Note also that **Customer A (direct) at 16%** and Distributor A at 45% are
disclosed separately, and Marvell does not say whether Customer A's product also
flows through Distributor A. If they overlap, true end-customer concentration is
lower than 61% combined; if they do not, the top two counterparties are 61% of
revenue. **The filings do not let us tell which.**
