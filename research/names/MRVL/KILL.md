# MRVL — KILLED

**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Trigger:** CLAUDE.md §4, Types A and B — *customer concentration >25% from one
customer with no multi-year contract*
**Status:** the name does not pass diligence today. This is a record of why, so
the work is not repeated in eighteen months.

---

## The trigger

**One disclosed counterparty is 45% of H1 FY27 revenue, with no contract.**

| Fiscal year | Distributor A share of revenue |
| --- | --- |
| FY21 | 13% |
| FY22 | 15% |
| FY23 | 20% |
| FY24 | 24% |
| FY25 | 34% |
| FY26 | **37%** |
| H1 FY27 | **45%** |

`[10-K FY26 0001835632-26-000011, Note 2; 10-Q 0001835632-26-000025 MD&A]`

Six consecutive years of increase, more than tripling. Threshold is 25%.

**And there is no contract behind it.** In the company's own words:

> "we typically sell products pursuant to purchase orders rather than long-term
> purchase commitments... cancel or defer purchase orders on short notice
> without incurring a significant penalty"

`[10-K FY26 0001835632-26-000011, Item 1A]`

That is the exact condition the criterion names: concentration above 25% **with
no multi-year contract**. Customer A (direct) is separately 16%.

## The nuance, stated because it cuts both ways

Distributor A is a distributor, not an end customer. The FY24 10-K described it
as "four module makers... supporting data center sales to hyperscale customers,"
which would mean real diversification sitting behind the single reported line.

**That description has not been refreshed since FY24.** Two years on, at 45% of
revenue, the composition of the fund's largest single revenue dependency is no
longer disclosed. So the mitigation cannot be verified, and relying on it would
breach the universal criterion — *the thesis requires trusting a number we
cannot tie to a filing*.

A concentration you cannot see through is not a smaller risk than one you can.

## Gate 1 also fails, independently

Type A requires ≥70% recurring or contracted revenue. The provable figure is
**0.5%** — $40.1M of deferred revenue against $8,194.6M FY26 revenue
`[10-K FY26, accrued liabilities note]`.

- RPO is not disclosed (practical expedient; contracts ≤1 year)
- "backlog" appears **zero times** in the 10-K
- services revenue is "insignificant"
- "substantially all" revenue is recognised point-in-time on product shipment

This is **FAIL, not UNKNOWN**. The disclosures establish it affirmatively;
there is nothing further to find.

## What the workup found that was good

Recorded so the kill is honest about what it is discarding:

- **Organic growth is better than reported.** FY26 organic **+45.3% [EST]** vs
  +42.1% reported — the automotive ethernet divestiture was a ~3.2pt *drag*, and
  no acquisition contributed (Celestial AI and XConn revenue "not material").
  H1 FY27 organic +36.3% [EST] vs +32.2% reported.
- **Gross margin rising**: 50.4% → 53.1% YoY in Q2 FY27.
- **Gate 3 passes, qualified.** On a clean base stripping the divestiture gain,
  FY26 FCF/NI **1.61 [EST]** — 1.28 tax-effected, 0.93 after also charging SBC.
- **Dilution bought growth.** Shares +5.84%, revenue per share +29.0%. Not tripped.
- **Leverage is modest.** Net debt ~$1,030.1M against $9,450.3M TTM revenue.

The business is growing fast at improving margins. It is killed on the shape of
its revenue base, not its trajectory.

## Two open items from base facts, now closed

- **Q3 FY26's $1,901.3M net income** — a **$1.8B pre-tax gain on the Infineon
  automotive ethernet sale** `[10-K FY26 Note 1]`. FY26's reported 32.6% net
  margin is confirmed not to be earnings power.
- **Q1 FY27's collapse to $34.5M** — a **$433.7M fair-value remeasurement of the
  Celestial contingent consideration liability** `[10-Q 0001835632-26-000025]`.
  This **recurs through FY29 and moves with the share price**, so it is a
  standing item to test each quarter against the serial-one-time-charges
  criterion (non-GAAP add-backs >15% of GAAP opex in 3 of 4 quarters).

## Further flags for any revisit

- **Segment disclosure was narrowed.** Four end markets collapsed into one
  ("communications and other") in Q4 FY26 with no stated reason. Removed
  disclosure is the highest-signal category in `/filing-diff`.
- **Data center is 79% of revenue**; **China 42% by ship-to destination**.
- **$283.0M of customer warrants** vesting as contra-revenue against
  **undisclosed** revenue milestones for an **undisclosed** customer.
- **Receivables**: Q4 FY26 revenue +22.1% YoY against AR +112.6%; DSO 51.5 →
  89.7 days (56.6 → 94.1 factoring-adjusted). Management attributes it to a
  back-end-loaded quarter and Q1 FY27 partially reversed. Cuts both ways — the
  51.5-day trough was flattered by $868.8M of FY25 factoring booked in OCF, and
  factoring is now shrinking ($412.0M H1 FY27 vs $526.8M H1 FY26), so recent OCF
  strength comes on *less* factoring. Monitor, not kill.

## Revisit if

- Distributor A's share falls below 25%, **or** a multi-year commitment is
  disclosed, **or** the composition behind that line is disclosed again and
  shows genuine diversification.
- The Celestial remeasurement stops recurring.
- Segment disclosure is restored.

## Note on the taxonomy

MRVL exposed a real gap: it has Type A margins (53.1% GM) with revenue that
sells sockets, not contracts. Forcing it into Type A produced a gate 1 FAIL that
is technically correct but analytically empty — a contract test applied to a
business that does not sell contracts, while the tests that would actually
separate a good fabless name from a bad one are asked by no column.

A **Type E · design-win / socket** shape is proposed in
`14-revenue-quality.md`. Worth noting: **MRVL fails Type E gate 1 too** — top-10
customer share is *rising*, 63→72→81→82% — but it would fail for the right
reason. The kill stands either way; the taxonomy still needs the fifth column.
